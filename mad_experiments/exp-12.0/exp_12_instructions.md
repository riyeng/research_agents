# MAD Peer-Review Architecture — Source-of-Truth Spec

## 0. Purpose

This document is the current source of truth for the next MAD architecture revision. It replaces earlier partially superseded discussion notes.

The immediate task is **architecture cleanup and redesign**. Do not yet optimize feature weights, journal-rank mappings, citation targets, survey design, or publication strategy.

Codex should **not rewrite the full script immediately**. First map the existing code to this specification, draft the new schemas/state transitions, identify deletions, and only then implement.

---

# 1. Research objective

MAD is an economics-specific multi-agent peer-review system for studying:

- adaptive specialist selection;
- independent expert review;
- expert disagreement;
- structured debate;
- revision of judgments after deliberation;
- qualitative usefulness of AI reviews;
- external validity of AI-generated research assessments.

The main scientific question is broader than whether MAD predicts journal tier better than a frontier generalist:

> What does structured, heterogeneous expert deliberation add to AI-assisted evaluation of economics research, when does it add value, and which dimensions of review change?

The system should remain scientifically useful even if debate produces modest average gains in publication-outcome prediction.

---

# 2. Three-layer separation

## 2.1 Architecture

The architecture should produce a rich, interpretable **raw review/debate record**.

It should contain only quantities with a clear deliberative meaning, such as:

- issue severity;
- issue/argument confidence;
- novelty-domain judgments;
- insight;
- repair scope;
- verdict;
- evidence;
- issue/argument links;
- chronological debate state.

It should **not** compute a final numerical paper-quality score.

## 2.2 Feature construction

A separate downstream script converts raw outputs into candidate paper-level features.

Examples:

- soundness / unresolved-issue burden;
- novelty breadth or consensus;
- insight summaries;
- repair-scope summaries;
- disagreement;
- pre/post changes;
- debate-mechanism measures.

Persona outputs do **not** need to be aggregated inside MAD.

## 2.3 External calibration

A later calibration layer maps constructed features to a chosen external outcome.

Possible targets:

- continuous journal prestige;
- pairwise journal ranking;
- citations;
- human-evaluated review quality;
- a user's field-specific historical target.

Pipeline:

`paper -> raw structured review/debate record -> feature construction -> external calibration`

Calibration must not reach backward into MAD. A benchmark result should not be used to hard-code persona weights, severity thresholds, novelty weights, or a final-score formula into the review process.

---

# 3. Architecture invariants

These are resolved decisions.

1. Select exactly three complementary expert personas.
2. Selection returns personas + rationale, **not numeric authority weights**.
3. PRE reviews are independent: a reviewer sees the manuscript and its own role, not peer reviews or the selection rationale.
4. Debate is issue-centered, not persona-centered and never targets `"paper"`.
5. Issue and argument IDs are generated by orchestration code, not invented by the LLM.
6. Debate rounds are synchronous: agents in round `r` see the state at the start of round `r`; they do not see same-round peer outputs until round `r+1`.
7. Round 2+ receives the full accumulated structured ledger/state, not an ever-growing verbatim transcript.
8. Persona outputs remain unaggregated in the raw result.
9. Soundness/validity is derived downstream rather than directly rated.
10. Severity is issue-level.
11. Repair scope is persona-level PRE/POST.
12. Confidence is issue/claim-specific, not global.
13. Novelty is a persona × domain judgment with explicit abstention.
14. Insight is a PRE/POST persona judgment without a separate confidence score.
15. PRE/POST verdicts remain as behavioral/editorial outcomes, not the latent quality score.
16. Plain-language issue statements are used for debate comprehension, not scoring.
17. Same-round non-response is never treated as resolution.
18. Late-raised issues are preserved but not treated as fully tested.
19. The raw history is append-only/versioned.
20. No dynamic reviewer-credibility updating.
21. No architecture-level final score.

# 4. Core constructs

## 4.1 Soundness / validity — derived downstream

Do **not** ask personas for a PRE or POST validity score.

Soundness should be derived later from the substantive issue record:

`substantive issues + severity + issue confidence + debate history/state -> soundness feature(s)`

Possible later constructions:

- maximum severity among maintained substantive issues;
- total confidence-weighted substantive issue burden;
- mean severity among maintained substantive issues;
- count/proportion of high-severity, high-confidence maintained issues.

Do not choose one formula inside MAD.

### What belongs in soundness

Primarily issues about whether central claims hold up:

- substantive empirical flaws;
- identification/inference failures;
- theoretical/formal inconsistency;
- data/measurement problems that undermine the central claim;
- computational failures that undermine the central claim;
- central overclaims where the headline claim exceeds what the analysis establishes.

### What does not automatically enter soundness

- novelty disputes;
- insight/framing weaknesses;
- optional extensions;
- future-work suggestions;
- minor rephrasals;
- stylistic issues.

Those belong to separate constructs or qualitative feedback.

## 4.2 Severity

Severity is an issue-level **conditional-damage** measure:

> If this issue is correct, how much damage does it do to the relevant central claim/contribution?

Retain a continuous 1–10 severity scale with semantic anchors.

Do not convert values such as 7 or 9 into mechanical score jumps.

Severity is not probability.

If debate makes an issue less believable, confidence/state should change, not necessarily severity.

If debate materially narrows the issue itself, preserve the original issue and create/version a narrowed issue rather than silently overwriting meaning.

## 4.3 Confidence

Confidence has one interpretation:

> How strongly is this specific proposition supported by the available evidence?

Use confidence only where that question is meaningful.

Required:

- initial issue confidence;
- claim-bearing `CHALLENGE` confidence;
- claim-bearing `DEFENSE` confidence;
- post-debate issue confidence for maintained/narrowed issues.

Not used for:

- global reviewer confidence;
- insight;
- repair scope;
- questions;
- concessions;
- the whole review.

Do not use a hard threshold such as `confidence < 4 -> abstain`.

Where a judgment can legitimately be withheld, use explicit abstention based on an evidence standard.

## 4.4 Stated novelty

Use **stated/manuscript-supported novelty**, not exhaustive global novelty.

Definition:

> How substantively novel is the contribution as supported by the manuscript's own positioning, evidence, and cited literature?

Domains:

- methodological;
- empirical;
- theoretical;
- policy.

Empirical novelty is first-class. It may arise from scarce/unique data, credible identification, a first-order question, or important new measurement/facts even with standard methods.

For each persona and domain:

- `HIGH`
- `MEDIUM`
- `LOW`
- `NONE`
- `ABSTAIN`

Each non-abstaining assessment includes a short explanation/evidence field.

`ABSTAIN` is **not** `NONE`, `LOW`, or zero. It means no defensible assessment from that persona.

Do not aggregate novelty inside MAD.

Four domains do not imply a maximum novelty score of 4.

Novelty challenges should use the same issue/argument graph, not a separate novelty-counter scoring system.

## 4.5 Insight

Insight remains a PRE/POST persona judgment about substantive intellectual illumination:

- framing;
- economic intuition;
- synthesis;
- argument architecture;
- connection between evidence and understanding.

No separate insight-confidence score.

Minor writing problems should not mechanically imply low insight or rejection.

## 4.6 Repair scope

Repair scope remains a PRE/POST persona judgment:

> What kind of work is required for the central contribution to hold up, given the reviewer's current understanding of the problems?

Candidate ordinal scale:

0. no substantive repair required  
1. local re-analysis/reframing within current evidence  
2. substantial work within current data/model/design  
3. new data/design/proof/core model required  
4. repair would effectively create a different paper

No repair-scope confidence.

If the reviewer genuinely cannot make a defensible judgment, permit abstention/uncertainty.

Repair scope is distinct from severity:

- severity = damage if an issue is true;
- repair scope = scale/type of work the paper needs overall.

## 4.7 Verdict

Keep PRE and POST verdicts.

Use them as:

- editorial summaries;
- behavioral outcomes;
- measures of debate-induced movement;
- comparison points with a frontier generalist.

Do not make verdict the primary numerical quality score.

Suggested qualitative semantics:

- `ACCEPT`: publishable with only minor/local changes;
- `RESUBMIT`: meaningful repair is required but the project remains substantially the same paper;
- `REJECT`: the central contribution does not hold up or credible repair would effectively require a different paper.

Novelty and insight should inform judgment, but avoid hard gates such as `LOW novelty -> REJECT` or `LOW insight -> REJECT`.

## 4.8 Constructive qualitative fields

Preserve:

- `structural_strength`;
- `best_case_contribution`;
- `revision_path`.

`structural_strength` and `best_case_contribution` are most useful in PRE and need not be regenerated POST by default.

`revision_path` should be comparable PRE/POST because debate may change the recommended path.

## 4.9 Plain-language translation

Preserve the layman-translation idea because prior iterations suggested jargon can cause reviewers to over-defer to other personas.

Every substantive issue should carry:

- a technical statement;
- a short plain-language statement.

This exists for debate comprehension only, not calibration.

Current bug to fix: `layman_translation` is elicited in the audit but dropped before debate.

Constructive fields:

- `structural_strength`
- `best_case_contribution`

Initial concerns are emitted as issue candidates rather than compressed into one `domain_audit`.

No validity/soundness score.
No global confidence.

## 5.3 `Issue`

Suggested fields:

- `issue_id` — engine-generated
- `dimension`
- `issue_type`
- `originator`
- `technical_statement`
- `plain_language_statement`
- `severity`
- `initial_confidence`
- `evidence` — specific manuscript-grounded support where available (e.g. section, equation, table, empirical claim, or data detail); if the issue concerns an omission, state why the missing evidence/analysis matters
- `round_created`
- `procedural_state`
- optional `post_confidence`
- optional `originator_final_stance` — exactly one of `MAINTAIN | NARROW | RETRACT | UNCERTAIN`
- optional `related_issue_ids`
- optional `supersedes_issue_id`

History is append-only/versioned.

## 5.4 `Argument`

Suggested fields:

- `argument_id` — engine-generated
- `issue_id`
- `persona`
- `action`
- optional `responds_to_argument_id`
- `content`
- `evidence`
- `confidence` only for claim-bearing challenge/defense actions
- `round_created`

Candidate actions:

- `CHALLENGE`
- `DEFENSE`
- `CONCESSION`
- `QUESTION`

Do **not** force every persona to emit every action type. Empty arrays are valid and preferable to invented criticism, concessions, or questions.

## 5.5 `PostAssessment`

Comparable core fields:

- revised `novelty_by_domain`
- revised `insight`
- revised `repair_scope`
- revised `revision_path`
- revised `verdict`

Where appropriate, issue originators also record:

- final stance on their own active issues;
- post-debate issue confidence.

POST should not silently introduce a new decisive flaw as though it had already survived debate.

## 5.6 `EditorOutput`

- integrated qualitative rationale;
- final editor verdict;
- author-facing letter;
- final revision path;
- constructive summary if useful.

No numerical calibration score.

# 6. Issue taxonomy and state

## 6.1 Issue types

A reviewer may raise zero, one, or multiple issues.

Candidate types:

- `substantive_flaw`
- `substantial_rephrasal`
- `rephrasal`
- `extension`
- `literature_dispute`
- `insight_weakness`
- `future_work`

Do not create a fake `no_issue` object. Zero issues is the correct representation.

Remove `barrier_category`. It mixed issue type with editorial consequence.

## 6.2 IDs

Issue and argument IDs are assigned by orchestration code.

The LLM should reference IDs it is shown, not invent them.

## 6.3 Duplicate concerns

Before creating a new issue, a debater should inspect the visible ledger.

If the same substantive concern already exists, use the existing issue through challenge/defense/concession/narrowing rather than duplicate it where possible.

No extra LLM deduplication call is desired.

If semantically equivalent issues still arise, preserve/link them and handle duplicate burden downstream. Duplicate wording must not mechanically make a paper appear less sound.

## 6.4 Procedural state, not hidden truth aggregation

Do not make one opaque final “truth status” another aggregation layer.

Prefer procedural states such as:

- `OPEN`
- `CHALLENGED`
- `MAINTAINED`
- `NARROWED`
- `RETRACTED`
- `LATE_RAISED`

The originator supplies a final stance of `MAINTAIN`, `NARROW`, `RETRACT`, or `UNCERTAIN`, plus post-debate confidence when the issue remains claim-bearing.

Downstream feature construction decides what counts as a “surviving” issue.

Do not also add `SUPPORTED/WEAKENED/REBUTTED` unless the exact adjudicator and rule are explicitly defined later.

## 6.5 Response opportunity

Rounds are synchronous.

Therefore an issue is not “surviving” merely because nobody answered it in the same round.

Non-response becomes meaningful only after a genuine later-round response opportunity.

If an issue is first raised in the final debate round:

- mark it `LATE_RAISED`;
- preserve it for editor/analysis;
- do not treat it as equivalent to a tested issue.

If POST discovers a genuinely new issue, store it as a late observation rather than settled soundness evidence.

# 7. Debate flow

## 7.1 Round 0 — selection

Input:

- manuscript context used for selection.

Output:

- three personas;
- selection rationale.

Temperature 0.
No explicit reasoning.

PRE reviewers should **not** receive the selection rationale; this avoids priming their independent assessments.

## 7.2 PRE audits

Each selected persona independently receives:

- manuscript;
- own role/profile;
- core review protocol.

Output:

- PRE assessment;
- zero/more issue candidates.

No reviewer sees peer outputs.

Temperature 0.

## 7.3 Debate Round 1

Each reviewer sees the same start-of-round state:

- all PRE assessments;
- all initial issues;
- manuscript debate context;
- technical + plain-language issue statements.

They may:

- challenge;
- defend;
- concede;
- ask issue-linked questions;
- propose genuinely new issue candidates.

Temperature approximately 0.35.

## 7.4 Debate Round 2+

Each reviewer sees:

- full accumulated structured ledger/state;
- article abstract;
- optional targeted manuscript excerpts relevant to active issues.

Do not provide only the immediately prior compressed round. Save raw verbatim outputs for research/debugging, but use the structured ledger/state as the canonical debate context.

If context becomes large, compress redundant prose **within issue records** while preserving issue identity and chronological state.

Temperature approximately 0.35.

## 7.5 POST reassessment

After the final debate round, each persona receives the accumulated ledger and updates:

- novelty by domain;
- insight;
- repair scope;
- revision path;
- verdict.

Issue originators also update final stance/post-confidence for their active issues where appropriate.

Temperature 0.

## 7.6 Editor

Recommended input:

- final issue ledger;
- POST assessments;
- sufficient manuscript/abstract context.

PRE/raw historical states remain saved for analysis, but should not automatically re-anchor the editor on criticisms already retracted/narrowed.

Output:

- integrated qualitative synthesis;
- editor verdict;
- author letter;
- final revision path.

Temperature 0.
No numerical score.

# 8. Expertise relevance and reviewer weighting

Selection itself is the architecture's relevance mechanism.

Remove:

- `.55/.30/.15` persona weights;
- dynamic reviewer credibility;
- global “winner” weights;
- persona × dimension weight matrices.

Why:

- expertise relevance varies by dimension;
- a single global weight is conceptually weak;
- weighting can be explored downstream if useful;
- debate should update arguments/issues, not global authority.

Do not replace reviewer credibility with another mandatory scalar called “argument salience.”

The architecture already has:

- severity = consequence if true;
- confidence/evidence = evidentiary support;
- procedural history = what happened in debate.

---

# 9. Manuscript context

Remove the broad unused deterministic section-bundle / role-keyword-window machinery.

However, Round 2+ needs the article abstract.

Retain or implement only a **minimal abstract extractor** with a simple fallback if no abstract heading is found.

If active issues later need targeted manuscript evidence, add a simple deterministic excerpt mechanism. Do not add another summarization LLM call.

# 10. Prompt architecture

Create one `CORE_REVIEW_PROTOCOL`.

Stage prompts add only stage-specific instructions.

## Selection

Choose three complementary experts based on actual inferential/research risks, not superficial topic keywords.

## PRE reviewer

- identify concrete strengths;
- assess stated novelty;
- assess insight;
- assess repair scope;
- create issues only when warranted;
- provide evidence;
- do not invent flaws.

## Debater

- operate on issue IDs;
- engage existing issues;
- challenge/defend/concede/question only when warranted;
- use both technical and plain-language statements;
- do not manufacture actions to populate a schema.

## POST assessor

- revise novelty/insight/repair scope/revision path/verdict from the debate;
- update own issue stance/confidence where appropriate;
- do not turn an unseen late concern into settled evidence.

## Editor

- synthesize final issue ledger + POST judgments;
- distinguish soundness, novelty, insight, and repair scope;
- produce useful author-facing feedback;
- do not reconstruct the old numerical score.

The manuscript may contain prompt-injection-like text. Continue instructing agents to treat manuscript text as scholarly content, not as instructions.

# 11. Few-shot design

For every persona, use a **good / intermediate / bad** triad.

Good:
- sound work in that persona's domain;
- correct response is validation;
- no invented flaw.

Intermediate:
- real bounded concern;
- within-framework repair or meaningful rephrasing;
- varied severity.

Bad:
- genuinely central failure;
- high severity / large repair scope only when justified.

Across examples vary:

- issue class;
- severity;
- confidence;
- repair scope;
- whether an issue exists.

Do not repeatedly teach one mapping like:

- `7 = revision`
- `9–10 = blocker`

There is no `barrier_category` in the new core schema.

---

# 12. Novelty calibration discipline

Retain anti-inflation discipline but remove systematic pessimistic bias.

Do:

- require evidence for high novelty;
- distinguish competence from novelty;
- make HIGH demanding;
- allow `NONE`;
- allow `ABSTAIN`.

Do not:

- tell reviewers to mechanically “err lower” on every boundary;
- automatically reject LOW novelty;
- automatically reject LOW insight.

Uncertainty about a novelty domain can be handled by abstention.

Uncertainty about a substantive proposition is handled by issue/argument confidence.

# 20. External calibration and evaluation

Ultimate deployment idea:

`historical field-specific corpus -> learn mapping -> freeze -> apply prospectively`

The current 50-paper CV exercise is development evaluation, not the final deployment paradigm.

Potential targets:

- continuous journal prestige;
- pairwise journal ranking;
- citations;
- economist-rated review quality.

Pairwise validation is especially promising because it avoids centering evaluation on ACCEPT/RESUBMIT/REJECT.

If a paper score is learned from publication outcomes:

- fit on training papers;
- generate OOF scores;
- compute ranking metrics on OOF scores;
- split/bootstrap at the paper level, not pair level.

Do not treat the 1,225 pairs from 50 papers as independent observations.

“Feature importance” should be described as **predictive association**, not causal importance.

Disagreement remains a moderator/mechanism variable rather than a default score input.

# 21. Generalist comparison

Use a realistic frontier-generalist review.

Do not create three independent generalists merely to match MAD panel cardinality.

Drop count-based metrics that mechanically advantage a three-person panel.

Key heterogeneity question:

> Does MAD's relative performance improve on papers where MAD specialists initially disagree strongly?

The generalist does not need its own disagreement measure.

> An empirical study of how AI systems should be structured to evaluate economics research, using specialist deliberation as the test case and producing an economics-specific review tool as a practical byproduct.

# 28. Acceptance tests

The new architecture should fail review if any of these are false.

1. Selection returns exactly 3 personas and no numeric authority weights.
2. PRE reviewers do not see peer reviews or selection rationale.
3. A reviewer can return zero issues without inventing `no_issue`.
4. Issue IDs are engine-generated and unique.
5. Argument IDs are engine-generated and unique.
6. Every argument references an existing issue.
7. No argument targets `"paper"`.
8. Same-round outputs are not treated as peer-visible.
9. Round 2+ receives the complete accumulated structured ledger/state rather than an ever-growing verbatim transcript.
10. Plain-language issue statements reach debate.
11. Final-round new issues are marked late/untested.
12. POST cannot silently turn a new unseen flaw into settled soundness evidence.
13. Every substantive issue is grounded in specific manuscript evidence where available, or explicitly identifies a consequential omission.
15. Severity is issue-level and not transformed into score jumps.
15. Initial issue confidence is present.
16. Challenge/defense confidence is present.
17. Questions/concessions do not require confidence.
18. Global reviewer confidence is absent.
19. Insight confidence is absent.
20. Repair-scope confidence is absent.
21. Novelty supports `ABSTAIN`.
22. `ABSTAIN` is distinct from `NONE`.
23. PRE/POST novelty, insight, repair scope, revision path, and verdict are saved per persona.
24. Issue history is append-only/versioned.
25. Dynamic reviewer credibility is absent.
26. Numeric persona weights are absent.
27. `barrier_category` is absent.
28. Architecture final score is absent.
29. Execution/novelty/insight penalties are absent.
30. Novelty floor/caps are absent.
31. `.60/.22/.18` scoring is absent.
32. Retracted/narrowed issues are not mechanically reinserted as unresolved.
33. Editor input prioritizes reconciled final ledger + POST state.
34. Model/temperature/retries are logged.
35. Raw output is rich enough to construct alternative downstream features without rerunning the LLM.

# 30. One-sentence architecture summary

> Select three complementary experts, collect independent structured assessments and issue candidates, run round-synchronous issue-centered debate over an append-only graph with technical/plain-language claims, collect POST judgments and issue stances, synthesize qualitatively with an editor, and defer all paper-level aggregation/scoring to downstream feature construction and calibration.
