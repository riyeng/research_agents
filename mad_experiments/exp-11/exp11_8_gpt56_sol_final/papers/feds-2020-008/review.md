## CONSTRUCTIVE SUMMARY
The paper has a potentially valuable descriptive contribution: it assembles maturity-specific Treasury-bill evidence and triangulates it across safety, liquidity, repo, and agency-note markets during two salient debt-limit episodes. The temporal and cross-market patterns are economically informative and plausibly consistent with information-sensitive debt, especially with greater avoidance of exposed CUSIPs in 2013. Novelty and insight are both moderate rather than high because the methods are standard, the evidence is associational, and only two episodes are observed.

The present version is not yet statistically sound. Its headline regressors vary at the daily level, while reported inference treats a much larger CUSIP-day panel without accommodating common-date or serial dependence. Correcting that inference could materially change the significance of the central safety, liquidity, and episode comparisons. The paper also overstates what contemporaneous projections and cross-market comovement can establish about causal components and equilibrium selection. These are serious but repairable issues. A revision that uses date-aligned inference, propagates uncertainty through the full decomposition, reports the relevant joint tests, removes mechanical liquidity overlap, and consistently frames the findings as reduced-form evidence could yield a credible and useful paper.

## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a descriptive, security-level event study of Treasury-bill dislocations during the 2011 and 2013 debt-limit impasses. Its principal contribution is the combination of maturity-matched bill–OIS comparisons with CDS, bid-ask, repo, and agency discount-note evidence. This structure documents economically meaningful covariation and offers suggestive evidence consistent with information-sensitive debt and greater screening of exposed CUSIPs in 2013. The setting and cross-market triangulation are promising, but the methods are conventional and the evidence comes from two episodes; novelty and insight are therefore both appropriately assessed as MEDIUM.

The consensus substantive flaw concerns inference. The principal safety, market-liquidity, repo, and event-interaction regressors vary primarily or exclusively by date but are repeated across 24,494 CUSIP-day observations. There are only about 750 dates and roughly 44 business dates across the two event windows. Heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors do not address same-date dependence across securities or serial dependence in daily shocks. The nominal panel size therefore cannot support the reported precision for coefficients identified by daily variation. This defect remains even if every causal claim is removed: the reduced-form significance of the headline associations has not yet been established. It is serious but repairable within the existing framework, and no hard blocker was identified.

Several related inferential issues must also be resolved. The manuscript should test the economically relevant sums of baseline and interaction coefficients: in Equation 1, the within-impasse safety effect is beta1 plus beta4 and the within-impasse market-liquidity effect is beta2 plus beta5. Conclusions cannot be based only on individual coefficient rows. Cross-episode comparisons, including the stated repo difference, likewise require displayed formal tests. The multi-stage procedure in Equations 2–4 uses generated coefficients and residuals; uncertainty must propagate through the entire pipeline while respecting date dependence. A date-block bootstrap that re-estimates every stage is the clearest remedy. The market-wide bid-ask measure should also be reconstructed leave-one-CUSIP-out, or checked against an independent measure, because it is built from the same bill panel used for the security-level outcomes and may contain mechanical own-observation overlap.

A separate substantial-rephrasal issue concerns the central interpretation. Contemporaneous CDS spreads, bid-ask spreads, and bill yields are jointly determined and may respond to common political and market news. Without instruments, timing restrictions, or exclusion restrictions, Equations 1 and 3 identify conditional projections rather than causal safety and liquidity effects. The residual-subtraction exercise is a statistical attribution, not a structural decomposition, particularly given the manuscript's acknowledgment that the components are non-additive and that safety adds no explanatory power on the peak 2013 day when both factors are included. Similarly, repo and agency evidence is compatible with collateral screening and pooling-versus-separating interpretations, but it does not formally establish equilibrium selection or exclude competing mechanisms. These qualifications must alter the abstract, introduction, results discussion, and conclusion, not merely appear in a caveat.

Minor rephrasal and documentation issues remain. The normalization passage says that the bid-ask spread is divided by its variance, whereas standardization uses the standard deviation. The implemented code must be verified. If standard deviation was used, correct the text; if variance was used, correct the scaling and all one-standard-deviation interpretations. A compact data-flow audit should report source observations, exclusions, unmatched merges, duplicate handling, OIS interpolation, maturity matching, and agency-note matching multiplicities. These points are not independent grounds for the decision but are necessary for reproducibility.

The defended critiques are appropriately bounded. The normalization inconsistency is likely a local documentation or scaling issue unless the code confirms a consequential implementation error. The equilibrium critique does not invalidate the empirical event patterns if the paper retreats to evidence consistent with, rather than proof of, the proposed mechanism. No new dataset, new episode, or structural model is required as a condition of revision; such additions would be extensions or future work. The minimum publishable contribution is a dependence-robust descriptive analysis showing which associations survive, accompanied by disciplined reduced-form language.

## OFFICIAL LETTER TO THE AUTHOR
Dear Authors,

The paper studies an important and unusually sharp setting in which the perceived safety of Treasury securities temporarily came into question. Its strongest feature is the integration of CUSIP-level Treasury-bill evidence with maturity-matched OIS rates, sovereign CDS spreads, bid-ask spreads, repo rates, and agency discount notes. This cross-market design has the potential to provide a useful descriptive account of the 2011 and 2013 debt-limit impasses and to show whether the observed patterns are consistent with theories of information-sensitive debt.

Our decision is MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. All three assessments recommend resubmission. There is no hard blocker, but the current inference does not establish the paper's headline statistical distinctions.

The central issue is the mismatch between the level of identifying variation and the reported standard errors. The main safety, market-wide liquidity, repo, and event-interaction variables vary by date but are repeated across many CUSIPs. The manuscript reports 24,494 CUSIP-day observations, yet the common factors have approximately 750 daily observations, and the two event windows contain only about 44 business dates. Heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors do not accommodate common shocks across securities on the same date or serial dependence in daily market conditions. The effective information for these coefficients is therefore much closer to the sequence of dates than to the nominal number of security-day rows. This concern applies even under a purely descriptive interpretation.

Please re-estimate the principal specifications using inference aligned with the identifying variation. A transparent approach would collapse outcomes to daily maturity buckets or fitted yield-curve measures and use time-series HAC or date-block bootstrap inference. Alternatively, a retained panel must use a dependence-robust procedure that addresses both same-date cross-sectional dependence and serial dependence. Given the short event windows, we encourage a date-block bootstrap as a central robustness procedure rather than reliance on ordinary date clustering alone. The full Equations 2–4 decomposition should be re-estimated within every bootstrap draw so that uncertainty from generated coefficients and residuals is propagated through all stages.

Please also report formal tests and confidence intervals for the quantities that support the economic claims. In Equation 1, the safety effect during an impasse is beta1 plus beta4, and the market-wide liquidity effect is beta2 plus beta5. ## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper exploits unusually sharp, security-level variation around two debt-limit impasses and triangulates the Treasury-bill evidence with CDS spreads, bid-ask spreads, repo rates, and agency discount notes. Matching each bill to an OIS rate of comparable maturity is a thoughtful improvement over comparisons with commercial paper, whose credit spreads may themselves react to an impasse.

**Domain Audit**: The empirical design supports a descriptive event-study interpretation, but the reported inference does not respect the level at which the identifying variation occurs. Equations 1, 3, and 5 are estimated on 24,494 CUSIP-day observations, while the principal safety and market-liquidity regressors—U.S. CDS spreads, Spanish-minus-U.S. CDS spreads, the average T-bill bid-ask spread, debt-limit-window interactions, and the repo rate—vary primarily or exclusively by date. Yet Tables 2 through 4 report only heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors. This treats the many same-day CUSIP observations as supplying substantially more independent information than they do and does not address serial dependence in daily market shocks. The problem is especially important for the headline interaction results, which are identified by roughly 44 business days across only two impasses, and for the claimed difference between 2011 and 2013. The appendix's daily erosion estimates similarly use a large cross-section of securities to estimate date effects, even though unobserved market shocks are likely correlated across all securities on a date. Consequently, the statistical significance underpinning the claims that safety matters at all times, liquidity matters only during impasses, and repo behavior differs across episodes is not established by the reported standard errors. Separately, the design does not isolate exogenous movements in CDS spreads or bid-ask spreads, so the estimated coefficients should be interpreted as conditional associations and decompositions rather than causal effects of safety and liquidity.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest defensible contribution is new descriptive evidence that Treasury-bill yields, sovereign-risk measures, and market-wide liquidity moved in a sequence consistent with information-sensitive-debt theory during the 2011 and 2013 debt-limit impasses, with especially strong security-level dislocations in 2013. The repo and agency-discount-note comparisons provide useful corroborating evidence that the ability to distinguish at-risk collateral may have contributed to the different market patterns across the two episodes.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Re-estimate inference at the date or date-by-maturity level. A direct approach is to collapse security-level outcomes into daily maturity-bucket or fitted-yield-curve measures and use time-series HAC or block-bootstrap inference. Alternatively, retain the panel but report inference robust to both same-day cross-sectional dependence and within-security serial dependence, supplemented by a date-block bootstrap that redoes the full two-stage decomposition. Report explicit joint and cross-episode coefficient tests, including the stated 2011-versus-2013 repo comparison. If the key interactions remain significant, retain the descriptive conclusions; otherwise narrow them to documented event patterns. Throughout the abstract and conclusion, describe CDS and liquidity coefficients as associations unless additional identifying variation is supplied.
