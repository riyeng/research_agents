## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper makes a novel and insightful theoretical point: welfare comparisons conducted near the deterministic steady state can conceal substantial state dependence in the gains from international monetary cooperation. Within the calibrated two-country New Keynesian model, net foreign assets organize the incomplete-markets results, while real-wage misalignment organizes the complete-markets results. The conditional-welfare design, Pareto analysis, and comparison of cooperative, nationally oriented, and flexible-inflation-targeting regimes are promising and potentially important. Novelty is high, and the economic mechanisms are generally clear.

The principal substantive flaw is numerical validation. The headline gains are negligible near the perturbation point and become large at substantial imbalances, yet the manuscript relies on pruned second-order perturbation without reporting nonlinear equilibrium residuals, policymaker first-order-condition residuals, approximation-order comparisons, pathwise or global benchmarks, or a validated state-space region. Pruning stabilizes simulations but does not establish approximation accuracy. Because the central magnitudes, welfare distribution, Pareto comparisons, and strategic rankings arise precisely in consequential tail states, this omission directly affects soundness rather than merely reproducibility. The record does not show that the results are wrong, and there is no unresolved blocker; it shows that their quantitative reliability remains unestablished.

A second substantive issue concerns commitment at transition dates. Equations (15) and (16) formulate commitment problems, but the conditional regime switch beginning after period 250 does not specify whether inherited Ramsey costates or policy promises are carried forward, reset symmetrically, or treated under a timeless-perspective convention. The comparison must isolate the policy regime rather than inadvertently combine regime change with repudiation of inherited commitments.

Two central claims require substantial rephrasing unless the analysis is expanded. First, the two-stage exercise is an objective-selection or delegation game: countries choose global versus national welfare objectives and then policies are jointly reoptimized in a new open-loop Nash equilibrium. It is not, as currently described, a unilateral deviation from a specified cooperative strategy with the counterpart's strategy held fixed, nor does it contain participation constraints, punishment, or enforcement. Claims about incentive compatibility and the fragility of a cooperative agreement therefore materially exceed the formal object. Second, transition states drawn from the calibrated cooperative ergodic distribution are model-generated or model-implied states; without comparison to observed distributions or a mapping from data into the full model state, they have not been shown to be empirically relevant.

Minor rephrasing is also needed for the institutional interpretation. The global-welfare benchmark does not model treaties, mandate changes, accountability, compensation, or enforcement. Net foreign assets and real wages should therefore be presented as model diagnostics or warning indicators, not sufficient operational triggers for formal coordination. These institutional additions are extensions or future work unless the authors retain claims about implementable coordination arrangements. Likewise, a full empirical mapping is optional if the empirical-relevance language is removed. The defended core contribution remains intact: the model can generate large state-contingent welfare wedges, and the mechanisms organizing those wedges are economically informative. The revision must establish where the numerical results are reliable and align the headline interpretation with the formal exercises.

## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Theorist Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper isolates the state dependence of international monetary-policy spillovers in a disciplined way. It holds the familiar two-country New Keynesian structure fixed, draws transition states from the model's cooperative ergodic distribution, and then compares cooperative and nationally oriented Ramsey allocations conditional on the same state. This makes the proposed mechanisms unusually transparent: net foreign assets organize the incomplete-markets results, while real-wage misalignment organizes the complete-markets results.

**Domain Audit**: The formal exercises support a narrower claim than the manuscript's headline policy interpretation. They show that model-generated transition states can produce large conditional welfare wedges in a calibrated workhorse model. They do not establish that the particular states generating the large wedges are empirically relevant or frequent: the baseline distribution is generated by calibrated technology shocks, an imposed NFA-stabilizing intermediation cost, and selected trade elasticities, without an empirical mapping from observed global imbalances or real wages into the model's state vector. In addition, the two-stage game does not assess deviation from a cooperative strategy in the usual incentive-compatibility sense. At stage one, countries are permitted to choose their objective functions; at stage two, all policies are reoptimized in a new open-loop Nash equilibrium. There are no specified cooperative strategies, unilateral policy deviations holding the other strategy fixed, enforcement arrangements, or punishments. Thus the exercise validly measures welfare incentives to select national rather than global objectives in this delegation game, but it does not by itself show that an international cooperative agreement is dynamically fragile. The quantitative state-contingent welfare result remains valuable, but the claims of empirical relevance and unilateral deviation from cooperation require material narrowing unless additional validation and game-theoretic structure are supplied.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest credible contribution is a state-contingent reinterpretation of the standard negligible-gains result. In a familiar open-economy New Keynesian model, comparisons made near the deterministic steady state can conceal large welfare wedges at model-generated states with substantial external imbalances or real-wage distortions. The paper also identifies how the relevant sufficient state changes with international financial-market completeness and shows numerically that pricing currency, portfolio denomination, and shock composition alter the size of the wedge.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Narrow the abstract, introduction, and conclusion to state that the paper establishes a quantitatively important possibility within calibrated model-generated distributions, rather than demonstrating that observed empirical conditions generate gains of the reported magnitude. Relabel the two-stage exercise as an objective-selection or delegation game and describe its payoffs as incentives to reoptimize under national welfare, not as incentive compatibility or unilateral deviation from a cooperative policy agreement. If the authors retain the stronger empirical and fragility language, they must instead map observed NFA and real-wage states into the model, document approximation reliability in the relevant tails, and formulate an explicit cooperative strategy/deviation/enforcement game.
