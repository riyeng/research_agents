## CONSTRUCTIVE SUMMARY
The paper's core contribution is a novel bond-level investigation of how a major securities-lending disruption relates to OTC corporate-bond liquidity, supported by rare linked microdata and a coherent analysis of dealer adjustment. Its strongest current result is compelling differential-event evidence: bonds with greater predetermined AIG exposure experienced reduced lending availability and activity, lower trading, wider client-dealer spreads, and greater interdealer reliance after AIG's program contracted. What remains unresolved is whether these patterns identify the causal effect of securities lending rather than portfolio-specific crisis shocks, changing outcome observability, intraday price movements, or missing-data conventions. The revision should focus narrowly on those issues. The mechanism evidence is promising but should remain suggestive, and broader welfare or regulatory analysis is future work rather than a present requirement.

## MINIMUM VIABLE REVISION PATH

## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper uses unusually rich linked data on corporate-bond transactions, securities lending, bond characteristics, and insurer holdings to study a large, plausibly supply-driven contraction in lending. Predetermined 2006 AIG exposure, bond and month fixed effects, characteristic-by-time controls, event-time evidence, and the analysis of dealer substitution make this substantially more informative than a simple before-after comparison. The empirical question is novel and important: whether securities lending supports liquidity in an OTC market and how dealers adjust when that source of supply disappears. Novelty is HIGH, while insight is MEDIUM. The institutional narrative connecting securities lending, dealer inventory, interdealer search, and client trading costs is coherent, and the evidence on increased interdealer activity is promising, though appropriately suggestive rather than independently causal.

The decision turns on substantive execution problems, not novelty or presentation. First, the causal counterfactual is not yet sufficiently established. The treatment intensity is AIG's predetermined portfolio share, and the shock occurs during a crisis with heterogeneous issuer, credit, underwriter, and dealer-network shocks. Bond and month fixed effects, observed characteristic-by-time controls, broad portfolio-distribution comparisons, and aggregate insurer-level sales tests do not rule out shocks correlated with AIG exposure. The manuscript's assumption that AIG and other insurers' bonds differ only along observable dimensions is especially demanding. The gradual contraction before the final shutdown also limits the reassurance supplied by leads concentrated near late 2008. Tighter issuer-time or same-issuer comparisons, fuller pre-period dynamics, and bond-level AIG holdings or sales controls are therefore necessary to sustain the causal headline.

Second, the central liquidity outcome may be endogenously observed. The realized spread requires qualifying dealer purchases and sales, the panel is explicitly unbalanced, and the baseline sample falls from 279,404 bond-months to 150,451 observations without an attrition decomposition. Because the paper also finds that AIG exposure reduced trading volume, treatment affects an input into whether the spread can be calculated. The observed post-shutdown sample may consequently differ in bond composition, trading sidedness, or trade size. The spread construction may also combine execution costs with intraday price movements because opposite-side transactions are not reported as time-matched or adjusted for within-day returns. These are substantive measurement and selection concerns affecting the headline estimate.

Third, unmatched Markit Securities Finance records are assigned zero availability and lending despite incomplete coverage. Without a coverage, identifier, and unmatched-record audit, missing reports cannot be assumed to be verified economic zeros. This issue bears directly on the documented lending first stage and mechanism evidence.

There is no separate substantial-rephrasal barrier in the record. The causal wording should, however, be made conditional on the revised evidence: if tighter comparisons and selection-robust analyses do not support the estimates, the paper must describe a differential liquidity response among AIG-exposed bonds rather than an identified causal effect of securities lending. The remaining minor rephrasal issue concerns policy scope. The study estimates a market-liquidity consequence of one unusually large, cash-collateralized lending-program shutdown; it does not estimate net welfare, identify an optimal regulation, or evaluate a concrete policy counterfactual. The conclusion should distinguish liquidity provision from collateral-reinvestment run risk and avoid generalizing to the shadow-banking system as a whole.

The critiques are reinforced by consensus across the econometric, data, and policy assessments; several were explicitly conceded, and none received a manuscript-based defense. There are no unresolved hard blockers, and the requested work is feasible in principle within the existing framework. Requests beyond the core identification, observability, spread-construction, and matching audits should be treated as extensions or future work rather than conditions for revision.

These are substantial requests because they bear directly on the central estimand, but they are developmental rather than fatal. The linked data and institutional setting remain highly promising. A revision that resolves the counterfactual, outcome-selection, spread-measurement, and matching issues would materially strengthen the paper.

## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper builds an unusually rich bond-level panel by linking TRACE transactions, Markit Securities Finance lending records, Mergent bond characteristics, and insurer statutory holdings. Its use of predetermined 2006 AIG portfolio shares, bond and month fixed effects, characteristic-by-time controls, an event-study presentation, and explicit checks of AIG's aggregate bond trading is substantially stronger than a simple before-after comparison.

**Domain Audit**: The central causal interpretation requires bonds with different 2006 AIG holding shares to have had parallel potential liquidity outcomes absent AIG's lending-program shutdown. The manuscript does not yet establish that counterfactual during a crisis that generated highly heterogeneous issuer, sector, underwriter, and dealer shocks. Equation (1) controls for bond fixed effects, month fixed effects, and observed bond characteristics interacted with time, but not issuer-by-month or similarly granular demand and credit shocks. The manuscript's third identifying assumption is especially strong: bonds held and lent by AIG and other insurers must differ only along observable dimensions. Similar broad distributions in Figures 4 and 5 do not establish this, and the event study in Figure 8 provides only a short set of leads near a gradually anticipated treatment. Moreover, Table 4 tests AIG's aggregate insurer-month sales rather than whether AIG disproportionately sold or stopped supporting the particular high-exposure bonds driving the treatment gradient. Thus, an AIG portfolio concentrated in issuers or dealer relationships that suffered differentially during the crisis could generate the estimated liquidity decline without a securities-lending channel. A related measurement concern is that liquidity is observed only when the trades needed to calculate the realized spread occur, while Table 2 itself reports lower trading volume for high-AIG-share bonds; neither bond-month selection nor changes in trade-size composition are addressed. The estimates are compelling differential-event evidence, but the current analysis does not fully separate the shutdown from contemporaneous portfolio-specific crisis shocks and endogenous liquidity-measure composition.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest credible interpretation is that the forced shutdown of AIG's securities-lending program provides a large, plausibly supply-driven natural experiment: corporate bonds with greater predetermined exposure to AIG subsequently became less available to borrow, were lent less, became more special, traded less, and experienced wider client-dealer spreads, while interdealer activity increased. Conditional on a valid within-portfolio counterfactual, this is important causal evidence that securities lending supports liquidity in OTC corporate-bond markets and that dealers only partially substitute toward interdealer trading.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Re-estimate the design using substantially tighter comparisons, preferably issuer-by-month fixed effects or matched bonds from the same issuer with different predetermined AIG shares, and report a full pre-shutdown event study covering the available 2006-2007 period. Add bond-level controls for AIG sales and holdings changes rather than relying on aggregate AIG sales. Demonstrate that the result is not generated by endogenous observability or trade composition using a common-support or balanced sample and liquidity measures adjusted for trade size or available for less-active bond-months. If the available within-issuer variation cannot support these tests, narrow the headline from a causal effect of securities lending to a differential liquidity response among bonds exposed to AIG's shutdown.

### Data_Scientist Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper builds an unusually rich bond-level panel by linking TRACE transactions, Markit Securities Finance lending records, Mergent FISD characteristics, and NAIC insurer holdings through CUSIPs. The baseline exposure variable is fixed in 2006, before AIG's lending-program shutdown, which avoids mechanical use of post-shock holdings in the principal design. Bond and month fixed effects, time-interacted bond characteristics, and two-way clustered standard errors further provide a disciplined empirical structure.

**Domain Audit**: The most consequential data concern is treatment-induced outcome observability. Liquidity is constructed from the difference between daily volume-weighted dealer sell and dealer buy prices, so it can only be calculated when the relevant bond has qualifying client trades on both sides. The manuscript explicitly describes the resulting data as a "monthly unbalanced panel," but does not report why bond-months enter or leave, how many eligible days underlie each monthly spread, or whether observability changes with AIG exposure after the shutdown. This matters because Table 2, column 6 finds that the shutdown itself reduced spot-market trading volume for AIG-exposed bonds. The treatment may therefore change whether the liquidity outcome can be measured, producing post-treatment sample selection that can bias the central difference-in-differences coefficient. The unexplained decline from 279,404 bond-months in Table 1 to 150,451 observations in the baseline regressions reinforces the need for a complete attrition and merge audit. A related missingness rule should also be audited: unmatched TRACE-MSF observations are assigned zero availability and zero lending even though the stated MSF coverage is less than complete. That rule conflates absence of a matched report with an observed economic zero and could affect the claimed lending first stage if missingness varies across bonds or time. These issues are repairable with the existing sources, but the manuscript currently does not establish that the headline liquidity estimate is invariant to its observation and missing-data pipeline.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest credible version is a causal, bond-level estimate showing that the externally driven shutdown of AIG's securities-lending program reduced liquidity for corporate bonds to which AIG was disproportionately exposed, followed by evidence that dealers partly substituted toward smaller and more dispersed interdealer trades. The contribution combines scarce microdata with a natural experiment to connect the securities-lending and OTC spot markets.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Provide a complete observation-flow and merge table by year and AIG-exposure quantile, distinguishing true economic zeros from missing or unmatched MSF records. Define precisely the minimum trades and trading days required to calculate each bond-month spread and report whether liquidity-outcome observability changes differentially after the shutdown. Then rerun the central specification on a sample whose eligibility is fixed using pre-shock information, such as an always-observed or predetermined-trading-frequency sample, and with alternative liquidity outcomes that remain defined in sparse or one-sided trading months. If the liquidity coefficient remains similar and the zero-imputation results survive treating unmatched MSF records as missing, the central conclusion would be substantially more credible.
