## CONSTRUCTIVE SUMMARY
The paper's contribution is potentially publishable but presently incomplete. Its promising elements are the fixed-window treatment of revisions, the organization of Tealbook estimates by forecast horizon, and the application of a factor stochastic-volatility model to an unobserved object. Novelty and insight are both MEDIUM: the method is an established one used in a useful new setting, and the backcast-nowcast-forecast comparison yields a clear economic narrative.

The decisive problems are repairable rather than fatal. ## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper carefully distinguishes backcasts, nowcasts, and forecasts and uses a fixed 12-quarter revision window rather than declaring the latest vintage to be truth. This is a strong design choice for a latent object that is never observed, and the horizon-specific factor stochastic-volatility framework is well matched to the real-time Tealbook panel.

**Domain Audit**: The central descriptive estimand is not consistently measured. Equations (3) and (4) specify e_it as an autocorrelated measurement error, e_it = rho_i e_i,t-1 + u_it, with exp(h_it) equal to the conditional variance of the innovation u_it, not of the level measurement error e_it. Nevertheless, Figure 3 labels exp(h_it/2) as the standard deviation of the measurement error and uses lambda_i^2 exp(h_ft)/(lambda_i^2 exp(h_ft) + exp(h_it)) as a variance decomposition. With estimated rho_i near 0.9 for several horizons in Table 3, innovation volatility can be much smaller than level-error uncertainty. Under a locally fixed volatility approximation, the level variance is exp(h_it)/(1-rho_i^2), and with stochastic volatility it must instead be calculated recursively or from the posterior predictive distribution. The same distinction affects the stated noise-signal ratios because exp(h_ft) is also the innovation variance of an autoregressive factor. Figure 5 apparently simulates the measurement-error process and may therefore be closer to the desired level uncertainty, but the manuscript does not reconcile that calculation with Figure 3. This matters directly for the headline claim about time-varying, horizon-specific uncertainty. Separately, the VAR evidence identifies recursive innovations and conditional dynamic associations, not exogenous output-gap or uncertainty shocks. Table 4 itself shows substantial correlations between the output-gap innovation and productivity, monetary-policy, and financial-uncertainty measures. Thus statements that uncertainty 'produces' or 'generates' macroeconomic effects require either a stronger structural identification argument or causal-language narrowing, and VAR confidence intervals should ideally propagate posterior uncertainty from the estimated latent innovations.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The paper provides a useful model-based reconstruction of the common component in Federal Reserve staff output-gap vintages and documents how the reliability of Tealbook estimates varies across forecast horizons and over the business cycle. Its strongest defensible interpretation is descriptive: conditional on the factor model, backcasts and nowcasts are substantially more informative than medium-horizon forecasts, and innovations in the Tealbook-implied common component are associated with economically coherent movements in output, employment, wages, and policy rates.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Recompute every horizon-specific uncertainty statistic using the posterior distribution of the level errors e_it and the level factor f_t, accounting for their autoregressive dynamics and stochastic-volatility paths. Explicitly distinguish innovation volatility, posterior state uncertainty, conditional forecast uncertainty, and unconditional level variance; then revise Figure 3, its decomposition, noise-signal ratios, and related abstract and conclusion claims. Explain why Figure 5's simulation-based intervals differ from the Figure 3 quantities. For the VAR section, characterize the responses as recursively identified conditional associations unless a credible structural-shock interpretation is supplied, and either integrate the VAR over posterior draws of the latent innovations or acknowledge that its reported intervals condition on generated regressors.

### Data_Scientist Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper makes a thoughtful data-construction choice by comparing estimates at a fixed 12-quarter maturation window rather than treating the latest vintage as truth. This avoids giving older observations mechanically more revision time and is well suited to an object, the output gap, whose true value is never observed.

**Domain Audit**: The central data panel is not documented consistently enough to verify that Tealbook observations have been assigned the correct target quarter, vintage, and forecast horizon. Tealbooks are produced about twice per quarter, while the state-space model indexes horizons and vintages quarterly. Figure 1 says that each quarter's nowcast comes from the first Tealbook produced in the subsequent quarter, but the manuscript does not state whether this rule governs the complete factor-model panel, how the other within-quarter Tealbooks are handled, or how irregular meeting dates and missing paths are mapped into horizons. More seriously, Section 3.1 reports 127 quarterly nowcasts over 1984Q1-2013Q3, although that interval contains only 119 quarters. This conflicts with the one-nowcast-per-quarter construction described for Figure 1.
