## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a novel partial-identification framework for first-price auctions that uses experimentally motivated overbidding inequalities rather than selecting a particular behavioral model. The valuation results are the manuscript's clearest contribution: rationality and RNBNE or RNBR overbidding generate sharp valuation-quantile bounds; variation in bidder counts can tighten those bounds; and the RNBR analysis gives the conventional GPV inverse a useful interpretation as a sharp upper valuation bound. This methodological novelty is HIGH, while the economic insight is MEDIUM: the architecture is coherent and the intuition for interior versus endpoint identification is useful, but the counterfactual analysis does not consistently distinguish oracle expressions from identified functionals.

The unanimous RESUBMIT assessments rest primarily on a substantive flaw in the seller-profit and reserve-price analysis. The lower expression in Section 3.1 uses the unknown cutoff v_n^{-1}(r), so it is not an identified functional of the bid distribution as written. The later plug-in discussion appears to substitute a bid-based cutoff without establishing that the resulting object preserves the claimed bound. More generally, Propositions 3, 6, and 8 compare profits after expanding or contracting participation regions, although the integrands contain payment minus seller value c. For r<c, added sales can have negative margins, so participation-set inclusion does not imply the asserted profit ordering. The numerical exercises with c=0 avoid this problem but do not establish the propositions on their stated domain. This defect propagates to the optimal-reserve analysis and requires revised definitions, domains, proofs, and estimators. A plausible repair is to work on r>=c, define observable bounds using bid or identified-set cutoffs, verify nonnegative margins at every set-comparison step, and establish whether restricting reserve optimization to r>=c is without loss under the maintained assumptions.

A second substantive concern is the relationship between the exact identifying restrictions and the laboratory evidence. Definitions 1 and 3 impose pointwise overbidding, yet the reported frequencies are only 74.4 percent and 71.8 percent for n=6, with violations concentrated among low valuations. The paper therefore cannot present the experiment as establishing the maintained population restriction. At minimum, the application must be framed as a conditional proof-of-concept or sensitivity exercise. A contamination-robust extension would be valuable, but the record does not establish that such a new theory is indispensable if the empirical claims are appropriately narrowed.

The manuscript also requires substantial rephrasing of central claims. The abstract groups valuation, seller-payoff, and reserve-price bounds as consistent with all models of overbidding and valid under heterogeneous strategies. The formal results are narrower: profit bounds require Assumption 2 or 3; level-k behavior is expressly excluded from Assumption 2; and general heterogeneous-strategy validity is not proved for upper profit or reserve-price bounds. Moreover, the symmetric pooled RNBR formula does not automatically extend to bidder-specific bid distributions. The revised paper must state separately which valuation, lower-profit, upper-profit, and reserve-price results survive heterogeneity and which require symmetry or additional counterfactual restrictions.

The empirical implementation also needs repair. RNBR depends on an estimated density, but the manuscript does not report the kernel, bandwidth, boundary correction, trimming, or leave-one-out treatment. These choices are consequential near bounded support and for the reported RNBR classifications. The repeated-subject structure and subject count should be disclosed, and bandwidth or tuning sensitivity is necessary for reproducibility. Claims that RNBR and RNBNE overbidding are 'equally prevalent' should remain descriptive absent dependence-aware uncertainty. The reviewers correctly defended the narrower point that a comprehensive bootstrap for every downstream identified set is an extension unless the paper claims formal confidence coverage; it is not a prerequisite for the population identification theory.

Minor rephrasing should also correct language such as 'the bounds are valid' when the evidence is graphical and conditional, and should describe the laboratory exercise as in-sample illustration rather than broad external validation. These wording issues are secondary to the mathematical repair. No unresolved blocker makes revision infeasible, and the valuation contribution remains promising. The appropriate outcome is therefore a constructive major revision rather than rejection.

## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Theorist Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper develops a coherent partial-identification architecture: pointwise restrictions placing bids between a benchmark strategy and valuations are translated into quantile bounds, then into counterfactual profit and reserve-price bounds. The distinction between RNBNE and RNBR overbidding is economically useful, and Proposition 4 clearly explains why RNBR overbidding is stronger under homogeneous monotone bidding but not generally under heterogeneity.

**Domain Audit**: The valuation-distribution results appear internally coherent, but the advertised profit bounds are not established as data-identified bounds for the stated domain of arbitrary seller value c and every reserve r >= 0. In Section 3.1 the proposed lower bound is pi_n(r) = integral over alpha >= v_n^{-1}(r) of [b_n(alpha)-c] plus c, which still depends on the unknown true valuation quantile v_n and therefore is an oracle inequality rather than an identified functional of the bid distribution. Section 5 then says an estimator is obtained by plugging the estimated bid quantile into pi_n, effectively replacing the unknown participation cutoff v_n^{-1}(r) by b_n^{-1}(r), but this replacement is not generally a valid lower bound when r < c. Since v_n >= b_n, the true counterfactual auction may allocate to additional types whose original bids are below r; their counterfactual payments are at least r, but if r < c these extra sales can reduce seller profit. The same sign problem affects upper bounds when the proof enlarges the set of participating types using an upper valuation bound: adding terms with payment below c need not increase profit. Propositions 3, 6, 8, and 10 therefore require either restriction to economically relevant reserves r >= c or a revised optimization-based treatment of allocation terms with possibly negative margins. This does not undermine the core valuation bounds, and the numerical and experimental profit illustrations set c = 0, so they operate in the safe region for nonnegative reserves.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest credible contribution is a new robust partial-identification method for first-price auctions that uses experimentally motivated overbidding inequalities rather than selecting a particular behavioral model. It produces sharp valuation-quantile bounds under RNBNE or RNBR overbidding, shows how variation in bidder counts tightens those bounds, and preserves much of the analysis under heterogeneous bidding strategies. Subject to a reserve-versus-seller-value qualification, it also yields economically useful bounds for seller profit and optimal reserve prices.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Restate and prove all profit and reserve-price results on the domain r >= c, explicitly redefine the observable lower profit bound using b_n^{-1}(r) rather than the unknown v_n^{-1}(r), and verify every step where an integral's participation region is enlarged or reduced using nonnegativity of payment minus c. Explain that restricting reserve-price optimization to r >= c is without loss under the maintained counterfactual bidding assumptions, or else provide a separate argument for r < c. Align the estimators in Section 5 with the newly defined identified bounds. The valuation-identification results need not be changed.

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper cleanly separates population identification from estimation and derives constructive bounds whose economic sources are transparent: rationality supplies the lower valuation bound, overbidding supplies the upper bound, and variation in the number of bidders tightens the identified set under explicit EP or SIV restrictions. It also distinguishes RNBNE overbidding from RNBR overbidding and states the additional counterfactual assumptions needed for seller-profit analysis in the formal propositions.

**Domain Audit**: The strongest defensible claim is conditional partial identification, not unconditional robustness of every reported bound. The valuation-quantile bounds are well aligned with the stated estimands and, under Proposition 11, remain valid with heterogeneous bidding strategies. The lower profit bounds also retain that robustness. However, the abstract says that the bounds for the valuation distribution, seller payoff, and optimal reserve price collectively "remain valid if different bidders employ different bidding strategies" and are "consistent with RNBNE and all models of overbidding."
