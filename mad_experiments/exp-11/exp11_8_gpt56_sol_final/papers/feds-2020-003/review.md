## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a disciplined theoretical and Bayesian evaluation of finite-horizon-like aggregate dynamics in a small New Keynesian model. Its principal contribution is the transparent block-recursive mechanism through which a forward-looking cyclical subsystem feeds slowly updating continuation values and thereby generates endogenous trends, persistence, and attenuation of distant expected policy-rate changes. The monetary-policy extension, trend-cycle characterization, and comparison with canonical, hybrid, exogenous-trend, and other behavioral models constitute genuine but bounded novelty. The consensus assessment is MEDIUM novelty and MEDIUM-to-HIGH insight: the mechanism is economically intelligible and the empirical horse race is useful, but the foundational finite-horizon framework and estimation methods are inherited or standard.

The paper is promising because it offers a parsimonious account of output and inflation persistence without habits or lagged-inflation indexation, nearly matches a more flexible exogenous-trend specification, and candidly reports that a homogeneous one-period-horizon model slightly outperforms the heterogeneous-horizon version. That last result is informative, although it limits what can be inferred about horizon heterogeneity.

The decisive substantive flaw is that the complete state system used for estimation has not been shown to be algebraically coherent and admissible. Equations (16) and (17) imply a loading proportional to (1-rho)Theta Gamma Phi. Equations (18) and (19), however, define Q as Theta Gamma Phi and then multiply Q by an additional scalar gamma, while the subsequent discussion and equation (21) treat Q as independent of gamma under equal gains. This creates an unresolved possibility that the learning gain is counted twice or that Q is incorrectly defined. The manuscript must state the exact transition law used in the likelihood, including the general case of distinct household and firm gains.

Even after correcting that algebra, equation (20) establishes determinacy only for the cyclical block. It does not ensure that Delta in equation (21) is nonzero or safely bounded away from zero, nor that the corrected trend-transition matrix has spectral radius below one. The reviewers supplied an admissible-prior example in which Delta is approximately 0.00045 and the trend transition becomes explosive despite satisfaction of the short-run condition. The manuscript does not document a complete admissibility region, rejection of invalid sampler draws, initialization under the relevant transition law, or posterior stability diagnostics. Because these conditions bear directly on the likelihood, posterior, marginal data densities, impulse responses, and historical decompositions, this is a substantive execution problem rather than a request for an extension. It is serious but repairable within the existing model; there is no unresolved blocker requiring rejection.

A separate substantial-rephrasal issue concerns the central behavioral interpretation. Aggregate output growth, inflation, and interest rates identify rho only conditional on the imposed common geometric horizon distribution and its common role in household and firm equations. They do not directly measure the fractions of actual households and firms with specified cognitive planning horizons. This limitation is reinforced by the reported result that the homogeneous one-period-horizon model slightly outperforms the heterogeneous specification. The defensible conclusion is evidence for finite-horizon-like aggregate attenuation under the maintained structure, not direct measurement of population planning-horizon shares. Although some debate participants viewed this as local calibration, the claim is prominent enough in the abstract and introduction to require substantial revision.

Minor rephrasal issues concern policy scope. Forward-guidance effects, historical descriptions such as policy being overly accommodative or behind the curve, and shock decompositions are conditional implications of the estimated model. The paper does not provide welfare analysis, optimal-policy design, credibility or implementation constraints, and therefore should not imply operational or welfare-based policy guidance. No such additional policy exercise is required; calibrated language is sufficient. The output-gap notation around footnote 9 and equation (15) should also be made consistent.

Defended critiques should be respected. The aggregate data can meaningfully discipline rho as a structural attenuation parameter within the maintained model, so the behavioral mechanism and model-fit contribution need not be abandoned. Likewise, the absence of welfare or institutional analysis is not a substantive defect because the paper can properly be framed as positive monetary-policy analysis. Prior-sensitivity or predictive-fit comparisons would strengthen model-ranking claims, especially where marginal-likelihood differences are modest, but these are extensions rather than the minimum basis for a favorable reassessment.

## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Theorist Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The manuscript derives a transparent block-recursive representation in which the forward-looking cyclical subsystem, equation (15), feeds a backward-looking belief-driven trend subsystem, equation (19). This makes the proposed persistence mechanism economically interpretable and permits direct comparison with canonical, hybrid, and other behavioral New Keynesian models.

**Domain Audit**: The strongest credible claim is that finite planning horizons combined with constant-gain updating can endogenously generate persistent model-implied trends and improve the fit of a small New Keynesian model. The main unresolved formal issue is equilibrium admissibility of the trend subsystem. The paper derives a modified Taylor principle only for the cyclical block in equation (20), then states that trend stability follows from 0 < gamma < 1 and 0 < gamma-tilde < 1. But equation (19) shows that trend stability also depends on the eigenvalues of the feedback matrix Lambda + (1-rho)gamma Q, which in turn depend on long-run policy coefficients and other structural parameters. In the FH-phi specification, the cyclical determinacy restriction does not constrain the distinct long-run coefficients. Admissible prior values can make Delta nearly zero and the trend transition explosive. For example, beta = 0.995, rho = 0.9, sigma = 2, kappa = 0.05, long-run phi-y = 0, long-run phi-pi = 0.8, and gamma = 0.1 yield Delta approximately 0.00045 and very large elements of Q, producing an unstable trend transition. The manuscript does not state that such draws are excluded, provide a general stability proposition, explain initialization under explosive trends, or report posterior stability diagnostics. Because the likelihood and marginal data densities rely on a well-defined state-space system, the estimation results are not fully validated until these admissibility conditions are documented and enforced. There is also a correctable notation inconsistency: footnote 9 defines cyclical output as y_t minus xi_t minus trend output, while the text following equation (15) appears to subtract xi_t again.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The paper provides a disciplined aggregate-data evaluation of Woodford's finite-horizon planning framework, extends it to a monetary rule with endogenous trend responses, derives a useful theory-based trend-cycle decomposition, and shows that short planning horizons plus slow continuation-value learning can reproduce inflation and output persistence without habits or lagged-inflation indexation. Conditional on formal stability and admissibility checks, the estimated model fits substantially better than the canonical and hybrid New Keynesian alternatives and nearly as well as a much more flexible model with three exogenous trends.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: State and prove the full admissibility conditions for both blocks, including non-singularity of Delta, cyclical determinacy, and stability of the trend transition matrix. Explain exactly how the sampler treats parameter draws violating these conditions, report the posterior distribution of the largest trend eigenvalue and its distance from one, and recompute the marginal data densities if unstable draws were not previously excluded. Also introduce one unambiguous output-gap variable, such as q_t = y_t - xi_t - bar-y_t, and use it consistently in equations (15) through (19) and the appendix.
