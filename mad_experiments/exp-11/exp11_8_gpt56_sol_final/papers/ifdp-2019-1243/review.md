## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper makes a useful contribution by placing a locally determinate, fundamentals-driven secular-stagnation equilibrium and a locally indeterminate, expectations-driven trap in a common representative-agent framework. This architecture clarifies why observationally similar stagnation episodes can imply opposite comparative statics and why equilibrium selection matters for quantitative model comparison. The reviewers consistently assess both novelty and insight as MEDIUM: the synthesis and comparative analysis are meaningful, although the principal ingredients and estimation methods build on established mechanisms.

The present execution is not yet sound enough for acceptance. First, the quantitative household objective reportedly contains -δB_t/(A_tP_t), while the analytical model, economic interpretation, and quantitative Euler equation use the positive bond-utility wedge. With bonds purchased through the budget constraint, these signs describe different economies. This is mechanism-critical because the positive wedge permits the negative natural rate underlying the secular-stagnation equilibrium. The intended sign appears clear and the problem may be typographical, but the authors must rederive the stationary equilibrium and verify from the estimation code which model generated the reported results.

Second, the formal analysis contains a verifiable curvature error. At the zero lower bound, equation (8) gives Y_AD=(1-β/Π)/δ and hence a negative second derivative with respect to inflation. The branch is strictly concave, not strictly convex as invoked in the proofs of Propositions 2 and 3. The propositions may be recoverable using correct concavity, endpoint signs, and any necessary slope conditions, but the current proofs are not valid as written. Because local determinacy is the paper's organizing distinction, the revised manuscript must also provide the omitted Jacobian, characteristic roots, Blanchard-Kahn conditions, and exact restrictions supporting the claimed equivalence between relative AD-AS slopes and determinacy.

Two central claims require substantial rephrasing. The Japanese exercise supports equilibrium-selection-dependent model comparison, not identification of a unique historical driver. The baseline preference for the BSGU specification depends on estimated sunspot-fundamental correlations, including a reported markup-sunspot correlation of 0.9273; imposing orthogonality or removing sunspot correlations reverses the ranking. The robust empirical insight is therefore sensitivity or non-identification conditional on equilibrium selection. Likewise, γ=1 fixes the aggregate nominal wage relative to its previous-period value. This is an aggregate nominal wage-floor commitment or complete downward nominal wage rigidity, not a conventional statutory minimum wage with a legislated level, coverage, compliance, heterogeneity, and employment reallocation.

Minor rephrasing is also needed for the broader policy discussion. Fiscal, wage, supply, and nominal-rate results should be described as conditional model comparative statics. The manuscript does not establish welfare gains, fiscal sustainability, distributional effects, institutional feasibility, credibility, or implementation robustness. These omissions are not demands for a new policy model; they delimit what the existing analysis establishes.

The critiques concerning the value of the quantitative and wage-floor exercises have been appropriately defended in narrower form. Conditional marginal likelihoods remain informative, and their reversal across equilibrium-selection assumptions is itself a useful methodological result. Similarly, a richer statutory-minimum-wage model is an extension rather than a prerequisite if the current intervention is accurately named. Future work could study heterogeneous implementation, welfare, alternative microfoundations, and institutional policy design, but these are not required for a viable revision.

**Domain Audit**: The principal formal problem is an internal sign contradiction between the quantitative household problem and the equilibrium system actually estimated. Section 3.1 specifies utility as containing "- δ Bt/(AtPt)" while saying that the household derives utility from holding bonds. With the budget constraint in which Bt is purchased by the household, that objective yields a bond first-order condition with a negative Euler wedge: schematically, 1 = βEt[...] - δ C_t^τ. Equation (13), however, uses 1 = βEt[...] + δ C_t^τ, matching the positive bonds-in-utility specification in Section 2. Thus the estimated equilibrium conditions do not follow from the displayed quantitative model. This matters because the positive wedge is exactly what permits the paper's negative natural rate and secular-stagnation steady state; taking the written objective literally reverses the mechanism. The intended model is evident and likely repairable by changing the minus sign to a plus sign, but the authors must rederive and verify the stationary first-order conditions rather than leave the structural model inconsistent with the estimated equations. A secondary proof issue should be corrected at the same time: at the ZLB, equation (8), Y_AD = (1 - β/Π)/δ, is strictly concave in Π, not strictly convex as repeatedly asserted in the proofs. The uniqueness arguments appear recoverable using the correct curvature and endpoint signs, but the written proofs are not correct as stated. Because local determinacy is the paper's central distinction, the omitted Jacobian/eigenvalue argument should also be supplied explicitly with its full parameter restrictions.

Its Japanese application further demonstrates that model ranking under indeterminacy depends materially on the equilibrium-selection rule and correlations between sunspot and fundamental shocks.
