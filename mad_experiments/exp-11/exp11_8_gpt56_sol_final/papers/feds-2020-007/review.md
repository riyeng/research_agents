The paper is promising as a carefully delimited descriptive analysis of completed FHA contracts. ## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a novel descriptive study of multidimensional pricing among represented high-LTV, 30-year fixed-rate FHA home-purchase originations in 2014–2015. By linking FHA administrative records, HMDA demographics and lender identifiers, and Optimal Blue rate-lock data, the paper shows that modest Black-white and Hispanic-white differences in realized interest rates coexist with opposite-signed differences in realized discount points. This is an important measurement contribution: interest rates and HMDA higher-priced-loan indicators alone are incomplete summaries of mortgage contracts. Novelty is HIGH because the linked data directly observe discount points at unusual scale in a post-crisis setting. Insight is MEDIUM: the multidimensional-pricing framework is useful and the progression from rates to points, lender revenue, fees, and HMDA classification is coherent, but the interpretation does not consistently distinguish realized contracts, offered menus, steering, and total economic cost.

The principal substantive flaw is an estimand and identification mismatch. Optimal Blue records the selected locked contract, not the complete menu offered to each borrower. Figure 4 and related regressions therefore characterize conditional relationships among realized contracts. They do not establish that racial groups faced identical offered schedules. Selection of a rate-point bundle may reflect liquidity, negotiation, loan-officer advice, steering, or unavailable alternatives; footnote 12 expressly acknowledges that differential steering cannot be ruled out. The manuscript consequently cannot use similar realized conditional curves to establish equal treatment or little systematic discrimination. This critique was ultimately accepted across all three assessments. It does not invalidate the descriptive rate-and-point findings, but it requires the paper’s central empirical object and contribution to be redefined.

A second substantive flaw concerns the fee analysis. The inferred outcome is based on APR, which may include positive discount points and depends on benchmark-rate timing that is not observed at lock in FHA-HMDA. These concerns are material because Table 3 documents racial differences in points and the average inferred fee contribution is small. In addition, APR spreads are reported only above a threshold, leaving the fee analysis selected toward reportable, relatively high-rate 2014 loans. Observed lock dates and points in the linked sample could improve measurement but cannot recover fees for loans below the reporting threshold. The current evidence therefore does not support the broad claim of no racial or ethnic differences in total fees.

The linkage also requires stronger validation. The Optimal Blue analysis relies on approximately 158,000 links produced from quasi-identifiers and tie-breaking rules, with an estimated match rate of about 65 percent among presumed covered loans. The manuscript does not report candidate multiplicity, tie-break frequencies, estimated false-link rates, match rates by race and lender, or sensitivity to unique and stricter matches. These diagnostics are needed to assess whether linkage error or differential attrition affects the reported gaps.

Several central statements require substantial rephrasal. First, claims that borrowers “face identical schedules” must become claims about similar conditional point-rate relationships among selected, locked contracts. Second, the paper cannot infer market-wide nondiscrimination from within-lender estimates: lender fixed effects remove differences associated with sorting or access across lenders without identifying why that sorting occurs. Third, lower points do not necessarily “offset” higher rates in present-value cost or welfare terms. Economic equivalence depends on duration, refinancing or prepayment, discounting, and liquidity valuation. Unless the authors construct explicit cost measures under stated assumptions, the evidence should be described as opposite-signed differences in contract components. Fourth, the title, abstract, introduction, and conclusion must be limited to the represented FHA-originations population and must distinguish the smaller linked sample and selected fee subsample.

Defended critiques and extensions should be kept separate from required repairs. The paper need not add rejected applicants, conventional or VA loans, service quality, or complete-menu data if it adopts the narrower realized-contract estimand. Those are important boundaries and future research directions, not prerequisites for a properly delimited descriptive paper. Conversely, complete offered-menu or experimental evidence would be necessary if the authors wished to retain claims about equal offers, steering, or nondiscrimination. Minor wording changes alone are not the issue here; the required revision changes the central interpretation and repairs or removes an important component analysis.

## OFFICIAL LETTER TO THE AUTHOR
Dear Authors,

The manuscript has a strong and unusually novel empirical foundation. Its most persuasive contribution is to show that mortgage pricing is multidimensional: among the represented FHA originations, modest racial and ethnic differences in realized interest rates are accompanied by opposite-signed differences in realized discount points. The linked FHA-HMDA-Optimal Blue data make this result possible at a scale not ordinarily available, and the evidence provides a useful warning against treating interest rates or HMDA higher-priced-loan indicators as sufficient measures of realized mortgage pricing.

Our decision is MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The paper’s core descriptive evidence remains promising, but its principal interpretation currently exceeds what the data identify.

The central issue is the distinction between realized contracts and offered menus. Optimal Blue records the rate and points associated with a selected, locked loan; it does not record every alternative offered to each borrower. Figure 4 therefore estimates a conditional relationship among realized contracts, not the point-rate schedule each borrower faced. Because the selected bundle may reflect liquidity, negotiation, loan-officer advice, steering, or unobserved menu availability, similar conditional curves cannot establish that racial groups received identical offers. This limitation is especially important because the manuscript itself acknowledges that differential steering cannot be ruled out. Accordingly, the claims that groups “face identical schedules” and that the results provide little evidence of systematic discrimination are not supported by the current design.

A successful revision should redefine the estimand throughout. The paper can credibly state that, among matched FHA originations, observed racial and ethnic rate gaps coexist with opposite-signed point differences and that conditional point-rate relationships among selected contracts are similar within lenders. It cannot infer equal offered menus, equal access, absence of steering, or nondiscriminatory treatment. The title, abstract, introduction, empirical interpretation, and conclusion should all reflect this distinction.

The fee analysis presents a separate measurement and selection problem. APR may incorporate positive discount points, and the relevant benchmark rate should correspond to rate setting even though the lock date is unavailable in FHA-HMDA. Since the paper finds racial differences in points and the average inferred fee component is small, these sources of contamination cannot be assumed innocuous. Moreover, APR spreads are observed only above the reporting threshold, so the analysis applies to a selected 2014 subsample. Reconstructing fees in the linked Optimal Blue sample using observed points and lock dates would improve measurement, but it would not recover fees for loans below the reporting threshold. You should either provide a substantially cleaner analysis with explicit sensitivity checks and restrict its interpretation to the reportable-APR subsample, or remove the broad claim that total fees do not differ by race or ethnicity.

Please also strengthen validation of the Optimal Blue linkage. At minimum, report candidate multiplicity, tie-break frequencies, match and attrition rates by race and lender, and sensitivity to uniquely identified or stricter links. These checks are important because the point analysis depends on approximately 158,000 linked observations and the current appendix does not establish the extent or distribution of false links and unmatched selection.

Two further interpretive changes are necessary. First, lender fixed effects identify within-lender comparisons but do not establish that racial differences associated with sorting or access across lenders are benign. Second, lower upfront points should not be described as fully offsetting higher rates in total cost or welfare unless you provide an explicit present-value analysis under stated assumptions about duration, prepayment or refinancing, discounting, and liquidity. Without such an analysis, describe the estimates as opposite-signed differences in realized contract components.

Finally, delimit the population carefully. The evidence concerns represented high-LTV, 30-year fixed-rate FHA home-purchase originations in 2014–2015, with a smaller Optimal Blue-linked sample and a still narrower fee subsample. Rejected or discouraged applicants, other mortgage products, service quality, and unmatched originations are outside the design. We do not require you to study those additional margins, but the manuscript must not imply that it resolves them.

The revision path is substantial but feasible. The paper’s novel descriptive contribution can survive and become clearer if it is separated decisively from claims about offered menus and discrimination, and if the fee and linkage analyses are repaired or appropriately narrowed.

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The design substantially improves on interest-rate-only fair-lending comparisons by merging FHA administrative records, HMDA demographics and lender identifiers, and proprietary Optimal Blue rate-lock data containing realized discount points. Restricting attention to government-insured FHA loans also reduces the scope for unobserved credit risk to explain within-lender racial pricing differences, while the specifications include FICO, income, geography, week, and lender fixed effects.

**Domain Audit**: The paper credibly documents descriptive differences in realized mortgage contracts, but the empirical design does not identify the central claim that racial groups "face identical schedules" or establish an absence of discriminatory treatment. Optimal Blue records the rate and points on the selected, locked contract, not the full menu offered to each borrower. Consequently, Figure 4 and Table A2 estimate conditional relationships among chosen contracts, E[points | selected rate, race, controls, lender], rather than race-specific offered point-rate schedules. Conditioning on the selected interest rate is conditioning on an endogenous choice potentially affected by liquidity, negotiation, loan-officer advice, steering, unobserved borrower attributes, or race-specific menus. Identical conditional curves among completed loans can therefore coexist with discriminatory steering or differential access to menu options. Lender fixed effects remove average differences across lenders but not differential treatment within lenders. The manuscript acknowledges that it "cannot rule out" steering, yet still concludes that borrowers "face" the same schedule and that the results provide little evidence of systematic discrimination. A related estimand problem is that a lower upfront payment does not mechanically establish that a higher interest rate is fully offset in welfare or total cost: equivalence depends on expected loan duration and discounting. The analysis supports the narrower statement that, among matched FHA originations, observed minority-white interest-rate gaps are accompanied by opposite-signed differences in realized points and that realized point-rate relationships look similar conditional on lender and observed characteristics. The fee evidence is narrower still because Table 6 uses only 2014 borrowers with rate spreads of at least 20 basis points and an APR-derived measure that can include positive points and timing error.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest defensible contribution is a nationally broad, post-crisis descriptive measurement result: among matched FHA loans originated in 2014-2015, black and Hispanic borrowers select or receive slightly higher-rate, lower-upfront-cost contracts, while the realized exchange between points and rates is very similar across racial groups within lenders. This demonstrates why interest-rate gaps and HMDA higher-priced-loan indicators alone are insufficient statistics for racial differences in multidimensional mortgage pricing.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Recast the headline conclusion throughout as evidence about realized contracts rather than offered menus or the absence of discrimination. Replace statements that groups "face identical schedules" with statements that their observed contracts trace similar conditional point-rate relationships, and state prominently that the design cannot distinguish borrower demand from steering, negotiation, selection into completed loans, or race-specific unobserved offers. Define the estimand explicitly; avoid saying that point differences fully "offset" rate differences without specifying a duration or present-value criterion; restrict the no-fee-gap conclusion to the analyzed high-rate 2014 subsample; and report inference robust to correlation within lenders as well as geography. If the authors retain the stronger discrimination claim, they would need data on complete offered menus or credible exogenous or experimental variation in offers and steering.
