## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a novel property-level, border-based evaluation of how the 2008 and 2011 conforming-loan-limit changes affected financing and owner-occupancy transitions for relatively expensive properties in selected adjacent ZIP codes. The local comparison and border-by-quarter fixed effects are promising, and the distinction between financing substitution and creation of homeowners is economically useful. Novelty is high: the combination of local policy variation with buyer-and-seller occupancy information and cash transactions is distinctive. Insight is medium: the economic narrative is clear, but the manuscript currently conflates four objects—CLL eligibility, actual guarantees, quarterly occupancy flows, and the homeownership stock.

The reviewers agree on three substantive flaws. First, Equation (4) measures quarterly net transitions into owner occupancy, whereas the abstract, Sections 4.6 and 5, and the conclusion make claims about the homeownership-rate stock. Table 4 pools flows and Figure 7 reports quarter-specific flows; neither supplies cumulative stock effects or confidence intervals based on the coefficients' joint covariance. Small or individually insignificant quarterly effects do not establish that the cumulative effect is economically modest. Second, CoreLogic does not observe guarantee status. The first-stage outcome assumes eligible fixed-rate loans are guaranteed, so a CLL change can mechanically alter measured guaranteed principal. The reported 91.4 percent McDash correspondence is aggregate and does not validate actual take-up among newly eligible jumbo-conforming loans by episode and treatment side; the manuscript also does not display its claimed observed-status McDash estimates. This problem compromises the guarantee first stage and any guarantee-dollar interpretation, though it does not mechanically invalidate the independently measured reduced-form effect of CLL policy on occupancy transitions. Third, excluding every property that experienced foreclosure during the sample conditions the analysis on a potentially post-treatment event that may affect and be affected by financing and occupancy. The primary analysis must avoid this selection or demonstrate that the exclusion is determined before treatment.

A related identification issue is substantive. A reduced-form-to-first-stage ratio is not automatically the causal effect of an additional dollar of government guarantees. Such an interpretation requires an observed or credibly validated guarantee first stage and a defensible exclusion restriction separating actual guarantee take-up from other consequences of CLL eligibility, including private-credit and loan-sizing responses. Interest-rate or price responses may sometimes be mechanisms of guarantees rather than exclusion violations, so the manuscript should distinguish mediators from channels caused by eligibility independently of actual take-up. If the exclusion restriction cannot be defended, the paper should retain the estimand as the reduced-form effect of the broader CLL policy and treat any ratio only as a scaling exercise.

The central substantial-rephrasal issue is policy scope. The evidence concerns $500,000–$1,000,000 properties, selected county-border markets, historical 2008 and 2011 changes at relatively high CLLs, and the observed horizon. It does not establish the effects of abolishing guarantees, a contemporary nationwide reform, expected taxpayer exposure, welfare, distribution, private-market substitution, bank balance sheets, or general equilibrium. Nor does the manuscript provide enough legal analysis for the claim that the contemplated reductions can be implemented without Congress. The title, abstract, introduction, and conclusion must therefore describe the historical marginal CLL counterfactual rather than government guarantees generally. References to guaranteed principal must track whether the variable is eligibility-imputed or observed; neither is equivalent to expected fiscal exposure.

Additional defended critiques concern auditability rather than demonstrated error. The manuscript should identify the sales window used for county purchase-price-to-assessment scaling and freeze treatment construction using pre-treatment information. It should also document the dated tax snapshot, joins, deduplication, missingness, transaction ordering, occupancy initialization and updating, attrition, and validation. The record does not prove leakage or erroneous reconstruction, so these are targeted verification requirements rather than grounds for rejection. Finer assessed-value-by-quarter controls and inference robust to county or border dependence are also warranted checks. There are no unresolved blockers and no basis for rejection. Extensions involving nationwide welfare, fiscal risk, distribution, or equilibrium effects are future work, not conditions for a locally framed paper.

## OFFICIAL LETTER TO THE AUTHOR
Dear Authors,

The manuscript addresses an important question with a promising and potentially distinctive design. Its strongest contribution is a local quasi-experimental estimate of how historical marginal changes in conforming loan limits affected the financing and owner-occupancy transitions of relatively expensive properties near selected county borders. The adjacent-ZIP comparison, property-level data, and observation of both buyer and seller occupancy—including cash transactions—offer meaningful advantages. The paper also poses a useful economic distinction: expanded eligibility may change how purchases are financed without creating additional homeowners.

Our decision is MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. All three reviewers recommend resubmission, and their concerns converge on issues that are central but feasible to repair within the existing framework.

First, the homeownership estimand must be corrected. Equation (4) records quarterly net transitions into owner occupancy, not the owner-occupancy stock. Yet the manuscript interprets Table 4 and Figure 7 as evidence about the homeownership rate. Please estimate cumulative effects on the owner-occupied-property stock at relevant post-treatment horizons and report confidence intervals using the full covariance structure of the quarterly estimates. A direct stock specification would be a useful accounting check, particularly if missing observations, attrition, or denominator changes could disrupt the equivalence between accumulated flows and stock changes. The revised paper should state what economically meaningful adverse stock effects the estimates can exclude, rather than relying on failure to reject individual quarterly coefficients.

Second, the financing first stage requires observed-status validation. CoreLogic does not observe guarantees, and the current measure labels eligible fixed-rate loans as guaranteed. Because eligibility is itself changed by the CLL, this construction can mechanically generate measured first-stage movement. Please display the claimed McDash results using observed agency or FHA status under a specification and sample as comparable as possible to the main analysis. At minimum, report actual take-up and classification diagnostics for newly eligible jumbo-conforming loans separately by policy episode and treatment side. If coverage differs from CoreLogic, explain and assess the resulting selection. Terminology throughout must distinguish eligible principal, observed government-backed principal, and expected taxpayer exposure.

Third, reconsider the exclusion of properties that experienced foreclosure at any point during the sample. As written, this conditions inclusion on a potentially post-treatment event that may itself affect occupancy. The preferred analysis should retain these properties or restrict exclusions to information fixed before treatment. Report sample counts, whether treatment predicts exclusion, and the sensitivity of financing and cumulative occupancy results to alternative foreclosure rules.

Fourth, clarify the causal object. If you continue to claim an effect of government-guarantee dollars, you must define and defend the exclusion restriction, use an observed or credibly validated guarantee first stage, and report the reduced-form-to-first-stage estimate with appropriate uncertainty. Otherwise, frame the estimates as reduced-form effects of CLL policy. A ratio may still be informative as a scaling exercise, but it should not be called the causal effect of an additional guarantee dollar without the required assumptions.

Fifth, make the data construction auditable. State the transaction window used to calculate county purchase-price-to-assessment ratios and reconstruct treatment using only pre-treatment information. Document the tax snapshot dates, joins and deduplication, missing-value rules, transaction ordering, occupancy initialization and updating, sample attrition, and validation of reconstructed occupancy. These omissions do not establish that leakage occurred, but the central constructed variables cannot presently be verified. Please also add targeted checks using finer assessed-value-by-quarter controls and inference robust to dependence at the county or border level.

Finally, substantially narrow the headline policy claims. The design identifies historical marginal CLL changes for $500,000–$1,000,000 properties in selected border markets over the studied horizon. It does not estimate abolition of guarantees, nationwide equilibrium effects, expected taxpayer losses, welfare, distributional incidence, current private-market substitution, or bank-balance-sheet responses. The assertion that limits can be lowered without Congress also requires a developed institutional and legal basis; absent that, qualify or remove it. The title, abstract, introduction, and conclusion should present the findings as evidence relevant to comparable marginal CLL reforms, not as a complete evaluation of reducing government mortgage guarantees generally.

A successful revision would preserve the paper's strongest contribution while aligning its estimands, measurement, and policy interpretation. We would be willing to consider such a revision.

## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper uses a thoughtful local comparison: adjacent ZIP codes on opposite sides of county borders receive different conforming-loan-limit changes, while border-by-quarter fixed effects absorb shocks common to each local housing market. It also separately documents a strong first-stage response of mortgage guarantees and a reduced-form response of owner-occupancy transitions.

**Domain Audit**: The design credibly supports a local reduced-form claim about how marginal CLL changes affected quarterly owner-occupancy transitions among $500,000-$1,000,000 properties near selected county borders. However, the reported homeownership regressions do not directly estimate the policy effect on the homeownership-rate stock. Equation (4) defines the outcome as the quarterly net transition flow: +1 for investor-to-owner-occupant sales, -1 for the reverse, and zero otherwise. A policy-induced homeownership-rate difference at a later date is therefore the cumulative sum of these quarterly transition effects. Table 4 instead pools post-treatment quarterly flows, and Figure 7 reports separate flow effects, without presenting cumulative effects, their joint standard errors, or confidence bounds. Consequently, failure to reject each quarterly coefficient does not by itself establish that the cumulative effect is negligible. The manuscript also does not formally report the ratio of the homeownership reduced form to the guarantee first stage, despite describing the target as the effect of government guarantees, or provide confidence intervals for that ratio. Identification is otherwise plausible but local: CLL assignment remains related to county median prices, and the treatment intensity is mechanically related to exact assessed value within broad $100,000 value bins, so finer value-by-time controls and inference robust to county or border-level dependence would strengthen the causal interpretation.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest defensible contribution is a quasi-experimental estimate showing that marginal increases and decreases in high-level CLLs substantially changed the financing of relatively expensive properties near county borders, while producing little detectable change in quarterly net transitions into owner occupancy. This is directly relevant to reforms that marginally reduce CLLs from comparable levels, rather than to eliminating government mortgage guarantees generally.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Estimate and report the cumulative effect on the owner-occupancy-rate stock over each post-treatment horizon, using the full covariance matrix to construct simultaneous or joint confidence intervals and translating the bounds into economically interpretable percentage-point effects. Then formally define the guarantee estimand, report the reduced-form-to-first-stage ratio with weak-identification-robust or delta-method confidence intervals, and calibrate the headline and policy claims to those bounds. The same data can implement this revision; finer assessed-value-by-quarter controls and county or border-level clustering should be added as targeted identification and inference checks.

### Policymaker Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper separates the policy's first stage from its ultimate outcome: it estimates whether conforming-loan-limit changes materially alter government-backed lending and then separately tests whether those changes alter owner occupancy. This is a strong policy-evaluation structure because Table 2 documents a sizable financing response while Table 4 shows that this response does not translate into a robust homeownership response.

**Domain Audit**: The evidence supports a narrow, policy-relevant conclusion: for $500,000-$1,000,000 properties in adjacent border zip codes during the 2008 increase and 2011 partial reversal, marginal changes in conforming loan limits substantially changed the volume of newly originated purchase mortgages treated as government guaranteed, without a detectable short-run effect on property-level owner occupancy. The abstract and conclusion sometimes convert this local result into a broader claim that government guarantees themselves could be "considerably reduced" with modest effects on homeownership and that lowering current limits is an administratively available reform. That translation is too broad. The paper does not estimate a nationwide equilibrium counterfactual, expected taxpayer losses, guarantee fees or subsidies, effects under current underwriting and risk-transfer arrangements, or institutional responses by private securitizers and bank balance sheets. Its guarantee measure is mortgage principal treated as guaranteed when a loan is eligible, not the government's expected fiscal exposure. The manuscript also asserts that limits can be lowered administratively without developing the statutory constraints created by the house-price-linked limit formula or distinguishing discretion over acquisition policy from authority to override statutory limits. The authors acknowledge several of these limitations in Section 5.2, which means the empirical work remains useful, but the headline policy implication should be reframed as evidence about the historical marginal CLL changes rather than as a complete case for implementing a nationwide reduction today.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The paper provides credible quasi-experimental evidence that historically observed marginal expansions of government-backed mortgage eligibility around county borders mostly changed how relatively expensive home purchases were financed rather than whether the affected properties became owner occupied. This is directly informative about claims that high conforming loan limits are an effective homeownership instrument and suggests that a marginal limit reduction may have a relatively low homeownership cost in comparable market segments.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Narrow the abstract, introduction, and conclusion to the identified historical margin, property range, geography, and outcome horizon. Replace statements about reducing "government guarantees" with statements about reducing the principal volume of government-eligible or government-backed new purchase originations unless fiscal exposure is separately measured. Present the policy result as directional evidence relevant to, but not a full evaluation of, nationwide CLL reform. Add a concise implementation subsection distinguishing statutory maximum-limit formulas, FHFA/GSE acquisition discretion, and any legal authority required for the contemplated reduction; explicitly state that welfare, taxpayer-risk, liquidity, house-price, distributional, and other housing-stability effects are outside the estimated counterfactual. Reporting confidence bounds in policy units would also clarify what size of adverse homeownership effect the estimates can exclude.
