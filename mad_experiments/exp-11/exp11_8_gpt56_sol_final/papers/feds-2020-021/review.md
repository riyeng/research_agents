The paper has a viable core: a transparent-in-concept, multi-service historical accounting exercise showing rapid growth in digital use per dollar. That contribution is potentially publishable and is moderately novel and insightful. The present manuscript, however, treats its constructed use units as validated constant-quality output and then draws cost-of-living and policy conclusions that do not yet follow. Full data disclosure, alternative quantity specifications, normalization checks, benchmarking, uncertainty ranges, and calibrated interpretation are the essential revisions. ## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a constructed-data and accounting contribution that develops long-run expenditure-per-use indexes for fixed internet, mobile phone, cable television, and streaming services, then shows how substituting these experimental deflators would change measured PCE inflation and productivity. The framework is clearly organized, the separation of delivery modes is sensible, and the historical multi-service implementation is potentially useful. Novelty and insight are both MEDIUM: the application is substantive, but it extends existing direct-volume and weighted-unit-value approaches; the users-times-use-times-quality decomposition is intuitive, but the paper does not resolve the distinction among network traffic, access-service output, complementary content, and consumer welfare.

The unanimous reviewer judgment is RESUBMIT, and the substantive critiques were conceded or remained unanswered. The principal flaw concerns the estimand. Expenditure per transmitted bit is a coherent unit-value statistic, but the manuscript has not established that bits are homogeneous, constant-quality units of access-service output. Traffic varies with content mix, resolution, codecs and compression, advertising, autoplay, background activity, applications, devices, and consumer demand. The manuscript itself calls traffic derived demand for complementary online activities. Consequently, the reported smartphone and internet price declines, and the resulting PCE and productivity adjustments, may depend materially on the chosen denominator rather than identify official inflation bias. Comparable concerns apply to viewing hours and programming availability. In particular, using log program counts as cardinal quality levels creates a normalization problem: adding a constant preserves the stated marginal comparison but changes quantity growth and the resulting price index. No utility normalization, willingness-to-pay evidence, or sensitivity to alternative transformations is supplied.

A second substantive flaw is reproducibility. The estimates combine heterogeneous government, company, trade, consultancy, and audience-measurement sources using acknowledged 'myriad judgements,' yet the manuscript does not disclose the complete annual inputs, source vintages, household-versus-business and fixed-versus-mobile allocations, interpolation and missing-data rules, definition breaks, splicing procedures, transformations, or propagated uncertainty. These choices are constitutive of the headline estimates, not ancillary replication details.

The central substantial-rephrasal issue is the movement from an experimental usage-based deflator to claims about mismeasurement of the true cost of living and determinate policy consequences. The current evidence establishes a conditional accounting path under the authors' quantity convention, not a welfare-consistent cost-of-living index. Likewise, the statement that monetary policy would need to remain accommodative for longer does not follow without assumptions about the inflation target's interpretation, the reaction function, expectations, forecasts, and treatment of statistical revisions. These claims must be narrowed unless additional validation supports them.

Minor issues include a fiscal sign error and a period inconsistency. Holding nominal income fixed, slower inflation adjustment of tax thresholds generally increases, rather than moderates, bracket creep. The smartphone discussion also describes 2007–2018, roughly eleven years, while claiming sixteen years of annual doubling. The exact calculation window must be corrected or explained.

The critiques defended across the record are therefore: the measurement-validity concern is central rather than an optional robustness request; full data provenance is necessary for evaluating the constructed series; and policy conclusions must be distinguished from mechanical accounting implications. There were no unresolved blockers and no separate extension requests. Benchmarking against plan characteristics or hedonic evidence, exploring alternative quantity measures, and propagating uncertainty are required to support the present claims; broader welfare or institutional policy analysis beyond that can remain future work if the paper adopts a narrower experimental-deflator interpretation.

## OFFICIAL LETTER TO THE AUTHOR
Dear Authors,

The paper addresses an important measurement problem and offers a clear accounting framework for constructing alternative price indexes for four rapidly changing digital-access services. Its strongest contribution is the assembly of long-run expenditure-per-use measures and the demonstration that, under the proposed quantity conventions, digital-service prices fall much faster than official deflators, with consequential conditional effects on PCE inflation and productivity. The service-level decomposition and separation of fixed internet, mobile, cable, and streaming are promising features.

After considering the manuscript and the unanimous reviewer assessments, my decision is MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. There are no fatal blockers, but the central interpretation is not yet supported.

First, the paper must clarify and validate its estimand. Spending divided by transmitted data is securely interpretable as expenditure per bit. It is not automatically a constant-quality access price or cost-of-living deflator. Bytes respond to video resolution, compression, advertising, autoplay, background traffic, application and device design, content mix, and consumer demand. Some changes raise traffic without proportionate gains in access quality or welfare; improved compression can reduce traffic while preserving or improving the experience. This tension is especially important because the manuscript also describes traffic as derived demand for complementary online activities. Please either validate the preferred quantity measures against consumer-relevant service characteristics or recast the results as experimental expenditure-per-use indexes. At minimum, report sensitivity to alternatives such as users only, users times hours, capacity or speed, latency, reliability, data caps, plan characteristics, stratified traffic, and adjustments or bounds for compression and video intensity. Carry the resulting range through the digital-service, PCE, and productivity calculations.

Second, address the cable and streaming normalization problem. Treating log channel or library counts as cardinal quality levels makes index growth depend on the arbitrary level normalization. Please provide an economic normalization, supporting willingness-to-pay evidence, or sensitivity results for alternative transformations and constants.

Third, make the empirical construction fully auditable. Provide the complete annual input data, source dictionary and vintages, transformation and aggregation code, and explicit documentation of household/business and fixed/mobile allocations, joins, interpolation, missing values, source-definition changes, revisions, and splicing. Report uncertainty or sensitivity associated with consequential construction judgments. A spreadsheet available only on request is insufficient for a paper whose contribution rests on assembled series.

Fourth, revise the interpretation throughout. Unless the validation exercises support a welfare-consistent interpretation, replace claims that the estimates measure official inflation bias or divergence from the 'true cost of living' with the more defensible statement that they show the path of prices under a proposed usage-volume convention. Present the PCE, real-income, welfare, and productivity implications as conditional accounting scenarios. Similarly, the monetary-policy discussion should not state that accommodation necessarily must persist longer; any such implication depends on how policymakers interpret the revised statistic, the numerical target, expectations, forecasts, historical revisions, and the reaction function.

Finally, correct two local errors. Slower CPI indexation of nominal tax brackets generally increases bracket creep for a fixed nominal-income path, rather than making revenue increases more modest. Also reconcile the 2007–2018 smartphone interval with the statement that data per dollar doubled annually for sixteen years, and disclose the exact period used in the calculation.

A successful revision need not prove that one quantity convention is uniquely correct. It must, however, distinguish what is directly measured from what is assumed, disclose the construction sufficiently for replication, show how sensitive the headline results are to plausible alternatives, and calibrate the claims to the evidence.

## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Data_Scientist Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The manuscript gives the constructed index a clear accounting structure: service volume is decomposed into number of users, time per user, and quality per hour, while fixed internet, mobile, cable, and streaming are kept separate to avoid treating services supplied under materially different conditions as identical. This makes the intended transformations conceptually traceable and allows service-level contributions to be examined.

**Domain Audit**: The central measurement label is not yet supported. For internet and smartphone service, the paper treats transmitted bits as homogeneous units of access-service output and labels spending divided by bits a quality-adjusted price. Yet transmitted data are jointly determined by network access, downstream content, application design, encoding and compression, device behavior, and consumer usage. A transition from text to high-resolution video, autoplay, advertising, or a less efficient codec can increase bytes without proportionally increasing either access quality or consumer welfare; improved compression can reduce bytes while improving delivered quality. Moreover, the manuscript itself describes traffic as derived demand for complementary free activities. Thus the resulting series is securely interpretable as expenditure per transmitted bit, but not without further validation as a constant-quality price or cost-of-living index for the access service. This matters quantitatively because smartphone prices falling about 50 percent annually and internet prices falling about 41 percent annually drive the estimated PCE-inflation correction and its increasing trend. The problem is compounded by insufficiently reported provenance: the annual 1987–2018 histories combine Cisco traffic estimates, government statistics, company reports, Nielsen measures, trade sources, and consultancy data using 'myriad judgements,' but the supplied manuscript does not document source coverage, definitional changes, allocations between business and household traffic or fixed and mobile traffic, interpolation, missing-data treatment, revisions, joins, or uncertainty. These are not ancillary details for a constructed-data paper; they determine the numerator-denominator alignment and may materially affect the headline index.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The paper provides a new, transparent-in-concept family of expenditure-per-use indexes for four rapidly changing digital-access services and documents that these indexes decline much faster than official deflators, with the implied difference growing as digital services gain expenditure share. If the output measures and source harmonization are validated, this is an important measurement contribution concerning inflation, real consumption, and productivity.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Publish the complete annual input dataset, source dictionary, transformation code, and vintage information; document all allocations, joins, interpolations, missing-value rules, and breaks in source definitions. Then distinguish explicitly between a unit value per bit and a constant-quality access price. Validate the latter using within-service alternatives tied to the service actually purchased, such as connection capacity, speed, latency, reliability, data caps, plan characteristics, or appropriately stratified traffic, and report how content mix, video resolution, advertising, compression, and source-allocation assumptions affect the aggregate digital-service and PCE results. If proportional bits cannot be defended as access-service output, narrow the headline finding to expenditure per transmitted bit rather than claiming a measured correction to the true cost of living.

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The paper clearly defines a descriptive accounting exercise rather than relying on an implicit regression design: it constructs service-specific quantity indexes, divides nominal expenditure by those quantities, and then reaggregates the resulting deflators into PCE. Separating fixed internet, mobile, cable, and streaming also avoids treating materially different delivery conditions as one homogeneous item.

**Domain Audit**: The central estimand is not sufficiently validated for the interpretation placed on it. The proposed indexes measure expenditure per constructed physical-use unit, especially expenditure per transmitted bit for internet and smartphones. That is a coherent unit-revenue statistic if bits are homogeneous units of access-service output. It is not automatically a constant-quality price index or cost-of-living deflator. Traffic changes with household demand, complementary content and devices, video resolution, advertising and autoplay, compression technology, plan capacity, and congestion. Some of these changes can raise bytes without proportionately improving access-service quality, while improved compression can deliver the same consumer experience using fewer bytes. Likewise, hours watched and the logarithm of available programs combine utilization and option value with provider output. Consequently, the reported 50 percent annual smartphone-price decline and the 36-basis-point PCE adjustment may reflect the maintained quantity definition as well as price or quality change. The manuscript acknowledges omitted latency and reliability but offers no sensitivity analysis, uncertainty assessment, or empirical validation showing that its traffic-based quantity index tracks consumer-relevant service quality. This is not a causal-identification problem—the paper is descriptive—but it is a central measurement-identification problem. The estimates currently establish how PCE changes under the authors' proposed unit-value convention, not that official inflation is overstated by the reported amount.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest credible contribution is a new long-run set of expenditure-per-use indexes for four consumer digital access services, assembled from many public and industry sources, showing that physical service use per dollar—particularly transmitted data per dollar—grew dramatically faster than implied by official deflators. Conditional on treating the constructed traffic and programming measures as constant-quality output, the exercise implies an increasingly important downward adjustment to digital-service and aggregate PCE inflation.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Define the target estimand explicitly as either a producer-output/unit-revenue deflator or a consumer cost-of-living index and justify why each quantity component identifies that estimand. Provide the underlying annual series and aggregation formulas, then report sensitivity estimates using plausible alternatives: users only, users times hours, traffic with adjustments or bounds for compression and video intensity, capacity or speed measures, and alternative programming-quality weights. Benchmark the resulting indexes against plan-level hedonic evidence where available and carry the resulting range through to the aggregate PCE and productivity calculations. Unless these exercises validate the preferred specification, recast the headline PCE result as a conditional accounting scenario rather than an estimate of true inflation bias.
