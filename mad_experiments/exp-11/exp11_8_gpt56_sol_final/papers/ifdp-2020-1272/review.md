### CS_Expert Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The manuscript tightly integrates model solution and filtering rather than treating them as separate computational problems. In particular, the canonical two-regime representation in equation (30) is used to derive a proposal distribution that incorporates the current observation, and the numerical section compares filters at stated particle counts, measurement-error scales, runtimes, and MCMC acceptance rates.

**Domain Audit**: The central algorithm is not internally executable as written because several formulas conflict with the model and with the appendix. First, Section 3.3 defines X2,t = [1, yhat_{t-1}, zhat_t, dhat_t, ghat_t, eR,t]' and equation (29) includes +eR,t, but the displayed gamma_2 vector has only five entries and omits the required coefficient 1 on eR,t. This coefficient affects the kink and constrained decision-rule coefficients through equations (22)-(25). Second, equation (47) gives D_t^j with the factor |Sigma_u|^{-1/2}|varsigma I + Phi_eta' A_s' Sigma_u^{-1} A_s Phi_eta|^{1/2}. The appendix's equation (A.6), however, implies |varsigma Sigma_u|^{-1/2}|barOmega|^{1/2}, which produces a negative, not positive, one-half power on the latter determinant, together with the appropriate power of varsigma. Equivalently, the Gaussian predictive density requires |varsigma Sigma_u + A_s Phi_eta Phi_eta' A_s'|^{-1/2}. The main-text expression therefore cannot be the normalized predictive density. Third, Proposition 1 maps a sampled innovation into the state using stilde_t^j = Phi_0 + Phi_1 s_{t-1}^j + eta_t^j, omitting Phi_eta even though equation (30) requires +Phi_eta eta_t. These may be transcription errors rather than errors in the authors' implementation, especially because the appendix contains a more plausible determinant derivation, but they occur in the paper's principal executable algorithm. The manuscript also omits enough implementation choices to prevent exact reproduction, including the Smolyak approximation level and resulting grid size, optimizer and stopping tolerances, definitive resampling scheme, random seeds, and computational environment. Consequently, the reported likelihood accuracy, posterior estimates, and runtimes cannot currently be verified from the manuscript alone.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest credible contribution is a computational method for DSGE models with one occasionally binding constraint that combines continuous piecewise-linear decision rules with direct sampling from a conditionally optimal mixture of truncated Gaussian proposal distributions. If the displayed inconsistencies are merely transcription errors and the implementation used the correct formulas, the approach offers a meaningful reduction in particle-filter variance and MCMC persistence while remaining feasible on a single processor.

Rerun the likelihood-density, MCMC, and empirical calculations and state whether the corrected specification reproduces Figures 1-4 and Tables 2-3 within Monte Carlo error.
