## CONSTRUCTIVE SUMMARY
The paper asks an important and well-defined question and presents a coherent empirical workflow. Its cross-shock organization, horizon distinction, and distributional intuition are promising, and the recurring point estimates may ultimately provide useful evidence that average impulse responses understate adverse movements in the lower tail. The present manuscript, however, does not yet test the comparative claim on which its headline rests, and it understates uncertainty arising from generated quantile outcomes and estimated shocks. The reconstructed distributions also require basic feasibility and uncertainty diagnostics. These are substantive execution problems rather than fatal conceptual defects. The financial-conditions and policy conclusions additionally need careful calibration: the former is currently an accounting relationship rather than identified mediation, and the latter does not constitute a welfare assessment. With valid joint inference, validated distribution fitting, transparent replication details, and claims matched to the resulting evidence, the manuscript could make a credible medium-novelty, medium-insight contribution.

## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a useful empirical synthesis of growth-at-risk quantile regressions and local projections. It applies a common measure of conditional GDP-growth risk to several monetary, credit, and technology shocks, clearly distinguishes the four-quarter forecast horizon from the impulse-response horizon, and offers intuitive illustrations of how quantile responses map into distributional changes. These features support assessments of MEDIUM novelty and MEDIUM insight: the ingredients and shock measures are established, but their integration across several shock families is a genuine, bounded contribution.

The decisive substantive flaw is inferential. The headline claim that contractionary shocks disproportionately increase downside risk is a claim about differences between quantile responses, such as the 10th-percentile response minus the median or 90th-percentile response. The manuscript reports marginal Newey-West confidence bands for individual responses, not confidence intervals or tests for these contrasts. Separate bands cannot establish that responses differ. In addition, the local-projection outcomes are generated from first-stage quantile-regression estimates based on overlapping four-quarter-ahead growth, while the stated inference appears to address only second-stage serial correlation. It does not propagate common first-stage uncertainty across quantiles and, where shocks are estimated internally, apparently does not propagate shock-estimation uncertainty. All three reviewers ultimately agreed that this omission leaves the central comparative finding unestablished, although it remains repairable within the existing framework.

A second substantive issue concerns the reconstructed skewed-t distributions. The manuscript estimates quantiles separately and then states that four skewed-t parameters exactly match four adjusted quantiles at each horizon. It does not report quantile-crossing checks, admissibility or existence diagnostics, uniqueness or stability information, parameter constraints, fitting errors, convergence results, or sampling uncertainty for the reconstructed densities. Consequently, the density figures cannot yet bear strong claims about movements in the entire forecast distribution.

The principal substantial-rephrasal issue is the claim that financial conditions are the key causal channel. Because NFCI and current GDP growth are the only nonconstant predictors used to construct the fitted quantiles, their responses algebraically account for movements in those quantiles. Similarity between the NFCI and lower-tail responses is therefore informative as a predictor-based accounting decomposition, but it does not independently identify causal mediation. The causal-channel language must be removed unless the authors add a design that identifies mediation.

A minor rephrasal issue concerns policy interpretation. Responses to unexpected identified monetary innovations do not establish that deliberate or systematic tightening is undesirable. The paper contains no welfare criterion or assessment of inflation, credibility, financial-stability, or mandate tradeoffs. This does not invalidate the descriptive estimand and does not require an optimal-policy extension; it requires calibrated language.

The reviewers also defended the paper against overextension. A welfare model is not necessary for a descriptive tail-risk contribution, and a bootstrap is not the only acceptable inferential method: a valid stacked estimating-equation, influence-function, delta-method, or suitable time-series resampling approach could suffice if it propagates uncertainty through all relevant stages and supports joint cross-quantile inference. Likewise, incomplete data metadata are important for reproducibility but do not independently show that the data construction is invalid. No unresolved blocker or fatal design defect was identified.

## OFFICIAL LETTER TO THE AUTHOR
Dear Authors,

Your manuscript develops a clear and potentially useful framework for studying how conditional GDP-growth quantiles respond to macroeconomic shocks. Its strongest contribution is the integration of growth-at-risk quantile forecasts with local projections across several monetary, credit, and technology shock measures. The distinction between the forecast horizon and the impulse-response horizon is especially helpful, and the distributional examples provide useful intuition. The contribution is meaningful but bounded: it combines established methods and data rather than introducing a new estimator or identification strategy.

We invite a major revision because the current inference does not establish the paper's central claim. Saying that contractionary shocks disproportionately increase downside risk requires direct inference on differences between quantile responses—for example, the 10th-percentile response minus the median and the 10th-percentile response minus the 90th-percentile response. Marginal confidence bands for individual responses do not test these differences. Moreover, the local-projection outcomes are fitted quantiles generated from estimated first-stage coefficients, yet the manuscript describes only Newey-West correction in the second stage. The revised analysis must account for first-stage quantile-estimation uncertainty, its covariance across quantiles, overlapping forecast outcomes, and uncertainty from shock series estimated within the paper. It should report direct pointwise and preferably simultaneous or joint inference for the relevant cross-quantile contrasts over economically meaningful horizons. If those contrasts are not statistically distinguishable, the abstract, introduction, results, and conclusion must characterize the evidence as a recurring point-estimate pattern rather than an established disproportionate effect.

The full-distribution reconstruction also needs validation. Please document whether the adjusted 5th, 25th, 75th, and 95th percentiles remain ordered at every shock and horizon; explain the skewed-t fitting algorithm, parameter restrictions, convergence criteria, and fit tolerances; report failures or residual fitting errors; and provide uncertainty for the reconstructed distributions. Without these checks, claims about changes in the entire economic outlook are stronger than the evidence supports.

The financial-conditions interpretation requires substantial revision. Since NFCI and current GDP growth are the only nonconstant predictors entering the fitted quantiles, the response of each fitted quantile is algebraically related to the responses of those predictors. The current evidence can support an explicit predictor-based or accounting decomposition. It cannot establish that financial conditions are the key causal transmission channel without an independently identified mediation design. Please revise this claim consistently throughout the manuscript.

Please also narrow the monetary-policy language. The estimates concern unexpected identified innovations and do not provide a welfare ranking of deliberate or systematic tightening. Statements that policymakers should be especially wary of contractionary policy shocks should acknowledge that the paper does not evaluate inflation stabilization, credibility, financial stability, mandates, or other policy tradeoffs. We do not require an optimal-policy model; careful claim calibration is sufficient.

Finally, strengthen reproducibility by listing exact series identifiers and sources, vintages or download dates, transformations, timing and aggregation conventions, missing-data rules, shock normalizations, covariance or bandwidth choices, and effective sample sizes by shock and horizon. For imported shock series, state clearly whether inference is conditional on the supplied series; internally estimated shocks should be incorporated into the uncertainty calculation.

These revisions are substantial but feasible within the paper's present framework. A successful revision would preserve the paper's useful descriptive contribution while aligning its headline conclusions with the inferential evidence.

## ROUND 1: INDEPENDENT AUDITS

### Econometrician Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The design clearly separates the four-quarter forecast horizon h from the local-projection response horizon s, and it applies a common tail-risk estimand across several independently motivated shock measures. Using two monetary-policy measures and three technology-shock measures is a concrete strength because the qualitative pattern is not tied to one particular shock construction.

**Domain Audit**: The central claim that contractionary shocks "disproportionately increase downside risk" is not supported by the reported inference. That claim concerns contrasts such as beta_0.10,s minus beta_0.50,s, and potentially joint restrictions across horizons and shock measures. The paper instead reports Newey-West bands for individual quantile responses, with Figure 2 showing bands only for the median and Appendix Figure 5 showing separate bands for selected tails. Separate significance or confidence intervals do not establish that two responses differ. Moreover, q_tau,t is a generated dependent variable estimated in the first-stage quantile regression using the same sample, while the stated standard errors appear to account only for serial correlation in the second-stage local projection. They therefore do not propagate uncertainty in the estimated quantile-regression coefficients; nor, for shocks estimated by the authors' VARs, do they appear to propagate shock-estimation uncertainty. This is especially consequential because the headline result is about differences across estimated quantiles, whose first-stage errors are correlated. A joint bootstrap of the complete pipeline and direct confidence intervals or tests for tail-minus-median and tail-minus-upper-quantile responses are needed before the claimed disproportionate effect is established. Separately, the conclusion that financial conditions are the "key channel" is stronger than the presented visual comparison permits: because the fitted risk measure is constructed only from current GDP growth and the NFCI, resemblance between its response and the NFCI response is not an independently identified causal mediation result.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The paper provides a useful empirical extension of growth-at-risk analysis: it treats fitted lower conditional quantiles of four-quarter-ahead GDP growth as a time-varying risk index and uses local projections to document how that index and other fitted quantiles move following several conventionally identified macroeconomic shocks. The point estimates consistently suggest that adverse monetary, credit, and technology disturbances move the fitted lower tail more adversely than the center or upper tail, with financial conditions accounting algebraically and descriptively for much of that movement.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Bootstrap the full two-stage procedure, re-estimating the quantile regressions and local projections in every replication and, where feasible, re-estimating the internally constructed shocks. Report simultaneous or otherwise clearly defined confidence intervals and direct tests for beta_0.10,s minus beta_0.50,s and beta_0.10,s minus beta_0.90,s, including joint tests over economically relevant horizons. Carry the same uncertainty through the fitted skew-t distributions and check quantile crossing and fit existence. If the contrasts are not statistically distinguishable, narrow the headline result to a pattern in point estimates. Recast the NFCI result as an accounting or descriptive decomposition unless a formal mediation exercise identifies it as a causal channel.
