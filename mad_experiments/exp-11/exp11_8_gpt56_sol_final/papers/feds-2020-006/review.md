## CONSTRUCTIVE SUMMARY
The paper’s promising core is a systematic demonstration that standard computational workflows for incomplete-markets open-economy models can yield materially different answers, organized by a useful near-unit-root NFA mechanism. Its novelty and insight are credible but moderate. The present manuscript cannot yet attribute those differences to local versus global approximation because it changes model closure, discounting, borrowing limits, and shock representation simultaneously, and the richer-model global benchmarks lack sufficient convergence validation. A focused revision that decomposes these effects, validates the numerical benchmark, audits simulation uncertainty, and narrows the “true/exact” and global-superiority claims would preserve the paper’s strongest contribution while making its conclusions rigorous.

## EDITORIAL RATIONALE
Decision: MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED. The strongest credible version of the paper is a broad computational study showing that commonly used global and local workflows for incomplete-markets open-economy models can produce economically important differences in precautionary saving, NFA and net-export dynamics, spectral behavior, and outcomes under occasionally binding constraints. Its most useful organizing insight is that near-unit-root NFA dynamics can amplify small differences in decision rules and materially alter net-export persistence. The comparison across endowment, RBC, and Sudden Stops models, multiple local methods, and time- and frequency-domain diagnostics is promising. Novelty and insight are both MEDIUM: the contribution is a non-trivial synthesis and benchmark exercise using established models and methods, supplemented by useful analytical intuition, rather than a fundamentally new algorithm or theory.

The central substantive flaw is identification. The headline comparisons do not consistently hold the economic model and stochastic environment fixed. In the endowment exercise, for example, the global implementation uses beta=0.94, an explicit debt limit, and a five-state Tauchen-Hussey process, whereas the local implementation generally uses beta=1/(1+r), a DEIR closure, and the AR(1) process in its equilibrium conditions. Analogous differences arise in the richer models. Discounting, stationarity closure, borrowing limits, and shock distributions directly affect precautionary saving, tail risk, persistence, and constraint incidence—the outcomes on which the largest discrepancies are reported. The current evidence therefore establishes that common practical workflows can differ, but it does not identify local approximation error as the cause of the full gap.

A second substantive flaw concerns validation of the global benchmark. FiPIt operates on finite grids with interpolation, is acknowledged not to rely on a contraction mapping, and is not guaranteed to converge. Independent value-function validation is reported only for the endowment model, while no adequate grid-, interpolation-, or shock-discretization refinement is documented for the RBC and Sudden Stops applications. Euler residuals are informative but do not alone establish convergence, uniqueness, or invariance to numerical choices. This is not merely a wording issue because benchmark error could affect the numerical rankings themselves.

A third substantive concern is simulation and output validation. Because the paper emphasizes NFA autocorrelations above 0.95 and low-frequency differences, burn-in, simulation length, seeds or fixed innovations, independent replications, and Monte Carlo uncertainty are decision-relevant. The reported impossible correlation of 5.593 in Table 5 requires correction and indicates a need for automated range and consistency checks. The manuscript should also distinguish moments obtained from invariant distributions or quadrature from those obtained by simulation. Exact empirical series lineage is desirable for reproducibility, although the record supports treating it as secondary to model harmonization and numerical uncertainty because the empirical moments serve mainly as calibration inputs.

The principal substantial-rephrasal issue is the repeated description of FiPIt outcomes as “true” or “exact,” together with the broad conclusion that the findings favor global methods. Unless matched-model comparisons and stronger convergence evidence support those claims, the outputs should be described as converged numerical benchmarks, and conclusions should distinguish workflow differences, closure effects, shock-discretization effects, and approximation error.

Minor rephrasal issues include correcting the statement that beta(1+r)<1 means the rate of time preference is lower than the interest rate; under the manuscript’s definitions, the ordering is the reverse. The deterministic consumption-growth statement should be limited to unconstrained portions of the transition because the debt-limit multiplier alters the Euler equality when the constraint binds. The world-equilibrium argument for beta(1+r)<1 should also be stated for the relevant class of self-insurance environments rather than as a universal result for all multicountry incomplete-market models. The manuscript should reconcile whether its periodograms are parametric or nonparametric.

Defended critiques should be recognized. The comparison is not devoid of value merely because the closures differ: it can support the narrower and useful conclusion that widely used practical workflows deliver different answers. Likewise, finite grids and the absence of a contraction theorem do not invalidate FiPIt; they require stronger validation and more accurate terminology. No extension beyond the existing framework is necessary. The required work is a controlled decomposition and validation exercise, not a new model or research agenda.

The global and local exercises differ not only in solution method but also in discounting, stationarity closure, borrowing constraints, and shock representation. These ingredients directly affect precautionary saving, persistence, tail events, and the incidence and severity of binding constraints. The present results can therefore document differences between practical workflows, but they cannot attribute the full gap to local approximation.

The revision should provide a controlled decomposition. At minimum, solve the same DEIR-modified economy globally and locally under identical parameters, constraints, and probability law to identify approximation error. Separately compare global solutions of the original and DEIR-modified economies to identify closure effects. FiPIt is a finite-grid, interpolated numerical method, and the manuscript acknowledges that it is not based on a contraction mapping and that convergence is not guaranteed. ### CS_Expert Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The computational comparison is unusually broad and well structured: the same three classes of open-economy model are evaluated using moments, impulse responses, spectral densities, Euler-equation errors, and execution times. The manuscript also reports concrete hardware/software details, grid sizes, simulation lengths, and alternative local calibrations rather than relying on a single accuracy statistic.

**Domain Audit**: The central numerical comparison does not hold the stochastic model fixed across solution methods, while treating the global results as the true or exact benchmark. In the endowment model, the global solution replaces the AR(1) income process with a five-point Tauchen-Hussey Markov chain, whereas the local methods use the AR(1) process directly. In the RBC model, the global method uses symmetric two-point shocks and an 8-state Simple Persistence Markov chain, including the imposed restriction rho_A = rho_R, while the local methods operate on the VAR representation. Matching variances, correlations, and autocorrelations does not make these shock distributions equivalent in nonlinear models, especially for precautionary savings, tail events, and occasionally binding constraints. Thus some reported differences in mean NFA, crisis probabilities, premia, and spectral densities can arise from shock discretization rather than local-versus-global approximation. In addition, FiPIt is repeatedly described as producing an exact or true solution even though the manuscript explicitly states that its convergence is not guaranteed, uses finite grids of only 30 capital by 80 bond nodes in the RBC model, and supplies no grid-refinement or independent-solver validation for the RBC and Sudden Stops models. Small Euler residuals are valuable but do not alone establish global convergence, uniqueness, or invariance to grids and interpolation. These issues materially weaken the headline ranking of methods, but they are repairable within the existing framework by harmonizing innovations across simulations and adding numerical convergence validation.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest credible contribution is a systematic computational benchmark showing that commonly used local approximations can materially misrepresent wealth dynamics, precautionary saving, net exports, and occasionally binding credit constraints in three canonical incomplete-markets open-economy models. The paper usefully connects these discrepancies to the near-unit-root behavior of NFA and demonstrates that higher-order local methods often add little relative to first order under the studied calibrations.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Rerun the core comparisons with a common shock process and identical realized innovations wherever possible; otherwise isolate shock-discretization error by solving each method under progressively refined, matched discrete distributions. Add grid and interpolation refinement tables for FiPIt, report residual distributions rather than maxima alone, and independently validate at least selected RBC and Sudden Stops solutions using a second global algorithm or substantially refined grids. Replace references to exact or true global solutions with converged numerical benchmark solutions unless these checks establish the stronger claim. Release the complete code, parameter files, random seeds or fixed shock sequences, solver tolerances, interpolation choices, and scripts that generate every table and figure.

### Theorist Analysis

**Structural Strength**: The manuscript isolates a clear economic mechanism behind discrepancies across solutions: net foreign assets are near-unit-root, and because net exports are a quasi first difference of NFA, small errors in NFA persistence can generate large errors in net-export dynamics. Equation (7), the analytic local-rule expression in equation (9), and the parallel time-domain and frequency-domain exercises give the comparison more structure than a collection of numerical horse races.

**Domain Audit**: The central attribution to solution method is not yet identified because the global and local exercises generally do not solve the same economic and stochastic environment. In the endowment benchmark, the global model uses beta = 0.940 and an occasionally binding ad hoc debt limit phi = -0.51, whereas the baseline local model uses beta = 1/R = 0.944, a DEIR closure, and no corresponding occasionally binding debt limit. In the RBC model, the global solution again includes an ad hoc debt limit and beta R < 1, while local solutions use DEIR. In addition, global solutions replace continuous AR/VAR innovations with finite-state Markov processes—five income states in the endowment model and symmetric two-point shocks in the RBC and Sudden Stops models—whereas local solutions are formulated using the continuous shock processes. These distinctions are economically consequential for precautionary saving, tail events, and constraint incidence, which are precisely the outcomes on which the manuscript reports the largest failures. Thus the reported gap combines at least three objects: approximation error, the effect of the stationarity closure and borrowing limit, and shock-discretization error. The paper validly shows that the full practical workflows can deliver different answers, but it does not currently establish that local approximation itself causes the differences or that the global result is the exact solution to the same model. A decomposition is especially important for claims such as 'global methods pin down the true NFA autocorrelation' and the conclusion that the findings generally favor global methods.

**Best-Case Contribution**: The strongest defensible contribution is a systematic demonstration that commonly used implementations of open-economy models—global solutions of a beta R < 1 economy with explicit borrowing limits versus local solutions of stationarity-modified economies—can produce economically large differences in precautionary saving, NFA and net-export persistence, spectral densities, and constrained-equilibrium outcomes. The paper also supplies a useful analytic explanation for why near-unit-root NFA dynamics amplify small discrepancies and documents that first-, second-order, and risky-steady-state local solutions are often nearly indistinguishable beyond their first moments.

**Minimum Viable Revision**: Add a controlled decomposition in at least the endowment model and one constrained model.
